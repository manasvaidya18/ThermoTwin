ThermoTwin

A digital twin for every vaccine fridge. It predicts cold-chain excursions before the standard alarms fire, freezing first. It explains the cause in the health worker's own language, and it turns repeated alarms into verified repairs.

Research, part 1The problem, as the evidence shows it

What this means for ThermoTwin

Freezing is the silent killer

Vaccine vial monitors record only heat, so freeze damage leaves no visible mark. Only about 40% of service-level health workers understand the shake test. Freeze-sensitive vaccines include pentavalent, hepatitis B, HPV, PCV, TT/Td and IPV. ThermoTwin predicts both directions and treats freezing as the urgent one.

The standards leave a window open

Under default rules a fridge can sit above +8 °C for almost 10 hours, or below −0.5 °C for almost an hour, before any alarm. Forecasting the time to a WHO-grade alarm is the job the standards leave undone. WHO's own catalogue names "predictive failure responses" as the goal.

The data already exists

India's eVIN runs about 25,000 SIM loggers at about 29,000 cold-chain points, reading every 10 minutes and uploading hourly. Its published outcomes concern stock-outs, not excursions. ThermoTwin is an analytics layer on that telemetry, with a cheap add-on node where no logger exists.

Alerts alone do not fix fridges

In a Mozambique trial, SMS monitoring cut time in the WHO freeze-alarm state from 4–6 to 2 hours a month, but chronically broken fridges kept failing. Slow response, not alarm volume, is the bottleneck. So ThermoTwin diagnoses the cause and closes the repair loop.

Why now

From 2026 every vaccine refrigerator must carry a WHO-standard equipment monitoring system, and the WHO data standard (DS01.2) defines alarm codes and a JSON format for raw data. That gives ThermoTwin ready-made prediction targets and a vendor-neutral input format. Close to a billion people are served by health facilities with no or unreliable electricity, so power cuts are a constant, not an exception.

Research, part 2What exists, and the gap

Across the market, stationary monitors alarm on thresholds. Where vendors say "predictive", they mean shipments or supermarket maintenance over days to weeks. No vendor we found publishes forecast lead time or false-alarm rates.

Closest analogues (say this honestly)

Emanate PowerPath Temp (2016–17) used machine learning on power draw to learn defrost and door patterns and issue predictive alarms for hospital fridges; its current status is unknown. Predictive-maintenance vendors do condition monitoring for supermarkets. An ESP32 autoencoder paper (Harrabi 2024) detects vaccine-fridge faults but does not forecast. So we claim the combination, not the idea of prediction.

Our white space

Short-horizon excursion forecasting, freezing first, with automatic cause attribution and a measured false-alarm budget. It runs on low-cost, low-connectivity hardware and sits as a vendor-neutral layer beside the certified logger. It reads WHO DS01.2 JSON or 10-minute eVIN-style logs. We will publish our lead time and false-alert numbers, which no competitor does.

The window is closing

Sensitech bought Berlinger's monitoring business (2024). Copeland now owns Dickson (completed September 2026) and agreed to buy Bueno Analytics. Digi bought Jolt and launched SmartSense ONE with "predictive insights". Nexleaf has the largest LMIC fridge dataset and door sensors in the field. Any of them could add short-horizon prediction, so ThermoTwin's position is "open, vendor-neutral, measured, and built for India's existing telemetry".
