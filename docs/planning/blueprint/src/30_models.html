Deliberately not the core
  • A deep LSTM as the main model. No evidence of better lead time at this horizon, harder to explain, and a patent notes plain LSTMs lag during door disturbances. Kept as a baseline.
  • Unsupervised anomaly detection as the alarm. On a pharma cold-chain dataset the best detector reached F1 0.52; door openings and defrost cycles look anomalous. Used only as a secondary signal on twin residuals with doors and defrost masked.
  • An LLM for detection. LLMs reason about time series only slightly better than chance, and root-cause agents solve 11 to 21% of benchmark cases.
  • Time-series foundation models as the engine. None has been tested on fridge data or runs on a microcontroller. Chronos-Bolt is shown as a zero-shot baseline only.

Physics coreThe twin, in detail

Three heat-storing nodes connected by heat paths. The air responds in minutes, the stored product in hours, which is exactly why air-temperature alarms are noisy.

Air        C_a dT_a/dt = UA (T_amb - T_a) + G_door d(t) (T_amb - T_a) - h_ae (T_a - T_e) - h_ap (T_a - T_p) + Q_defrost(t)
Evaporator C_e dT_e/dt = h_ae (T_a - T_e) - eta P_comp(t)        P_comp from the current clamp; eta = effective cooling efficiency
Product    C_p dT_p/dt = h_ap (T_a - T_p)                          T_p is the Virtual Vial

Ice-lined refrigerator variant: add an ice-lining node with latent heat (holds near 0 C until melted)
   -> long holdover during power cuts, and a cold zone near the base that explains freezing risk

Cause list (fixed, so explanations stay honest)

Door left open · power loss · compressor or refrigerant · gasket leak · evaporator icing or missed defrost · thermostat drift (freeze) · high ambient · overload or warm restock · condenser fouling · sensor fault.

Repair verified

When a technician marks a repair done, the twin compares the relevant Vital over the next 24 hours with the unit's baseline band (90 to 110%). In band: "Repair verified" and the ticket closes. Out of band: the ticket reopens with the evidence. This turns Nexleaf's lesson (broken fridges keep failing) into a measurable loop.

Agent componentsThe bounded AI copilot

We use agents where the evidence says they work, and nowhere else. IBM's AssetOpsBench found the best maintenance agent finished 46.5% of tasks and agents claimed success they had not achieved in 23.8% of analysed runs. On real root-cause analysis, the best models solve 11 to 21% of cases. So detection and escalation are deterministic, and the LLM explains, translates and drafts.

Guardrail pipeline for every LLM output
  1 JSON schema check            2 every number must equal a tool value (template insertion, never free typing)

  5 any claimed action must match a tool return     6 log prompt, evidence, output, human action
  fail any step -> send the deterministic template instead

Why one orchestrator, not a swarm

AssetOpsBench found interleaving tool calls was more reliable than planning first and delegating to many agents. So ThermoTwin has one orchestrator with typed tools. The "agents" above are jobs, not autonomous actors.

Models and languages

Any capable API model with structured output (declare which), or a small open model on the gateway for offline use. Indian-language translation and speech through Bhashini or a cloud text-to-speech service. Only telemetry features leave the facility; no personal data goes to the LLM.

How we evaluate the copilot: 100% of numbers match tool outputs on 100 test alerts; 100% of causes from the allowed list; native speakers rate the Hindi and Marathi Cause Cards; staff rate usefulness on a 1 to 5 scale.
