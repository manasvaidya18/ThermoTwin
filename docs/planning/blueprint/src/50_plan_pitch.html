Slide 2 · Problem Statement and Target Need

Who is affected

  • Vaccine handlers and ANMs at about 29,000 cold-chain points in India's immunisation programme, district cold-chain officers, technicians, and the children who receive the doses.
  • Also pharmacies, blood banks and food cold rooms.

The current gap

  • WHO-standard alarms fire only after 10 h at or above +8 °C or 60 min at or below −0.5 °C. Damage starts first.
  • Freezing is the silent killer: Indian logger studies found vaccine below 0 °C for 10.5 to 14.8% of storage time, and two-thirds of tested DTP vials showed freezing. Vial monitors cannot show freezing.
  • Paper logbooks flagged deviations at only 5.6% of devices.

Why it matters now

  • From 2026 WHO requires equipment monitoring on every vaccine fridge and names predictive failure response as the goal.
  • India's 25,000 connected loggers already stream data, used today for stock, not prediction.
Visual: the "unalarmed window" chart from this page. Say: "The alarm the world uses for vaccine fridges waits an hour in freezing conditions before it rings. By then the vaccine is gone, and nobody can tell by looking."

Slide 3 · Proposed Solution and Innovation

What makes it different

  • Freeze-first, two-sided forecasting with three clocks: breach, WHO alarm, power-cut holdover.
  • A physics digital twin of every fridge, with ML learning what the physics misses.
  • Two-key alerts: ML and physics must agree before a phone rings, within a false-alert budget of 1 per fridge per month.
  • From alarm to verified repair: Cause Card, technician ticket, and the twin confirms the fix.
  • A bounded AI copilot that explains in Hindi or Marathi and drafts tickets, but never decides.

Core mechanism

Sense → Twin → Sentinel (risk) → Guardian (alert policy) → Copilot (explain and act) → Command (apps).

Why it is feasible in this timeframe

  • Software-only on simulated data, as Track 4 allows.
  • Proven, light models: RC grey-box plus LightGBM, which runs in microseconds on an ESP32.
  • Built on the WHO DS01.2 data format. [Built today: add your first result here.]

Slide 7 · Expected Impact and Scalability

Impact

  • Fewer frozen or heat-damaged doses, with warnings that come before the damage.
  • Fewer false callouts, measured as false alerts per fridge-month and published.
  • Faster repairs: diagnosis before the technician travels, verification after.

Scale

  • A software layer on telemetry that already exists: about 29,000 cold-chain points and roughly 85,000 to 107,000 pieces of cold-chain equipment (mostly ice-lined refrigerators and deep freezers) in India's public programme.
  • Small models run on a microcontroller, so it works offline and costs little per fridge.
  • Same engine for pharmacies, blood banks and food cold rooms; lack of refrigeration loses an estimated 12% of global food production (UNEP and FAO).
  • Model: per-facility subscription, or bundled with monitoring vendors and state programmes.

Viva preparationQuestions judges will ask

Why not just lower the alarm threshold?

That is what vendors' "warnings" do, and it adds noise. Lower thresholds still fire after the trend starts and fire on every door opening. We forecast, use product temperature instead of air, and require ML and physics to agree, under a stated false-alert budget.

Why focus on freezing?

Indian logger studies measured vaccine below 0 °C for 10.5 to 14.8% of storage time, and vial monitors cannot show freezing. WHO's own rules already treat freezing as more urgent (60 minutes versus 10 hours).

Your data is simulated. Why should we trust it?

We randomise equipment, seasons, power and faults, test on unseen fault combinations and on a second, independent generator, and state the simulator's limits. The twin learns each real fridge's physics online, so field use starts with a short calibration period.

What is new here? Monitoring already exists.

Monitoring exists; forecasting with cause and a measured false-alarm rate does not, in any product we found for stationary vaccine storage. The closest analogue (Emanate, 2017) targeted hospital fridges and its status is unknown. We publish our numbers; no vendor does.

Where is the AI, and why not a big deep-learning model?

The AI is the hybrid twin plus calibrated gradient boosting, chosen because the evidence favours it at a 15 to 60 minute horizon and because it runs on a microcontroller and explains itself. A TCN and a time-series foundation model are included as baselines.

Can an LLM hallucinate a wrong diagnosis to a health worker?

The LLM picks a cause from a fixed list, every number comes from a tool, a verifier checks each output, and if any check fails we send a template. It never decides whether to alert, and never advises using or discarding vaccine.

How do you avoid false alarms from door openings and defrost?

Product temperature instead of air, door and current signals, logged suppression windows with automatic re-arm after the unit's own recovery time, and the two-key rule.

What if the network is down?

The node keeps a local model and buzzer and stores 30 days of data; alerts fall back to SMS; data is forwarded when the link returns.

Is it a medical device? Is it WHO-certified?

It is an advisory layer beside the certified logger, which stays the official record. Humans decide on stock, following national guidelines. Regulatory status (CDSCO) is part of our next steps.

How will you measure success?

Recall on WHO-grade events, lead time over Nexleaf-style alerts, false alerts per fridge-month, true-alarm suppression below 1%, calibration, cause accuracy, and repair-verification accuracy.

How does it scale and make money?

It reads existing DS01.2 or eVIN data, so scale is software. Per-facility subscription, or bundled with monitoring vendors and state programmes.

CredibilityClaims to use, and claims to avoid

Use (with the source on the slide)

  • WHO alarms: 10 h at or above +8 °C, 60 min at or below −0.5 °C (WHO DS01.2).
  • 10.5 to 14.8% of storage time below 0 °C in Indian studies; two-thirds of tested DTP vials frozen (Murhekar 2013, Das 2020).
  • Logbooks flagged deviations at 5.6% of devices (Das 2020).
  • One in 20 facilities lost vaccine to temperature damage in a year (WHO EVM).
  • From 2026, monitoring required on all vaccine fridges (WHO EMS FAQ).
  • About 25,000 eVIN loggers at about 29,000 points (UNDP).
  • Twice-daily manual readings catch about 5 to 6% of excursions (CDC-associated simulation).
  • Root-cause agents solve 11 to 21% of benchmark cases, so detection stays deterministic.

Avoid

  • "Up to 50% of vaccines are wasted by cold-chain failure": an unreferenced 2005 sentence about total wastage.
  • "$35 billion lost by biopharma (IQVIA)": no IQVIA source found.
  • "75% of vaccines in India frozen": the study says two-thirds of tested vials.
  • "85 to 99% of cold-chain alarms are false": that is clinical bedside data; use only as an analogy.
  • "First AI cold-chain system", "WHO-certified", "99% accurate", "AI decides if vaccine is safe".
  • "Multi-agent AI runs your cold chain".
