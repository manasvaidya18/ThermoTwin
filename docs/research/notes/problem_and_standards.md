## Q1. Storage requirements, freeze-sensitive vaccines, and how common accidental freezing is

### Takeaway
WHO requires vaccines to be stored at +2 °C to +8 °C, and many of the most widely used EPI vaccines are permanently damaged by freezing. These include the pentavalent/DTP family, Hep B, HPV, PCV, IPV and TT/Td. Two systematic reviews (2007 and 2017) show accidental freezing is pervasive. Across 45 studies, exposure below the recommended range during storage was about 33% in wealthier countries and 37% in lower-income countries. Freezing is the bigger, less visible risk, because VVMs only detect heat.

### Cited Findings
**Storage ranges**
- WHO guidance asks programmes to verify that storage temperatures stay within +2 °C to +8 °C in cold rooms and vaccine refrigerators, and within −25 °C to −15 °C in freezer rooms and vaccine freezers. **[PRIMARY]** [WHO Vaccine Management Handbook VMH-E2, "How to monitor temperatures in the vaccine supply chain", WHO/IVB/15.04, July 2015](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04). I read the full-text mirror at [Logistics Cluster](https://log.logcluster.org/sites/default/files/2022-08/WHO%20-%20How%20to%20Monitor%20Temperatures%20in%20Vaccine%20Supply%20Chain.pdf).
- WHO's 2015 definition of a temperature excursion is "An excursion event during which a vaccine is exposed to temperatures outside the range(s) prescribed for storage and/or transport." The ranges come from the manufacturer's stability data. When cool water-packs are used in transport, excursions up to +20 °C are acceptable. **[PRIMARY]** [WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)
- The PQS E003 refrigerator standard says domestic refrigerators "should never be used to store vaccines". For standard compression refrigerators, "No freezing (i.e. temperature of 0 °C or less) is accepted" in the vaccine compartment. **[PRIMARY]** [WHO PQS Catalogue E003 Refrigerators and Freezers (category text "Latest version: September 2024"; catalogue version date 23 Sep 2026)](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e003_0.pdf)

**Freeze-sensitive vaccines (WHO Table 2, 2015)**
- More freeze-sensitive group: DTaP, DTaP-HepB-Hib-IPV (hexavalent), DTwP, DTwP-HepB-Hib (pentavalent), Hepatitis A, Hepatitis B, HPV, MenC conjugate, Pneumococcal conjugate (PCV), TT/DT/Td. **[PRIMARY]** [WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)
- Also damaged by freezing: inactivated cholera, inactivated influenza (split), Hib (liquid), IPV, typhoid polysaccharide, MenA conjugate (its diluent is freeze-damaged), rotavirus (liquid and freeze-dried) and yellow fever. **[PRIMARY]** [WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)
- Not damaged by freezing: BCG, Hib (freeze-dried), Japanese encephalitis, measles, MMR, OPV, rabies, rubella and varicella. All diluents must still be protected from freezing. **[PRIMARY]** [WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)

**Prevalence of freeze exposure (systematic reviews)**
- Matthias et al. 2007 (Vaccine 25(20):3980–6), the first systematic review, concluded that "accidental freezing is pervasive and occurs across all segments of the cold chain." Between 14% and 35% of refrigerators or transport shipments exposed vaccine to freezing temperatures. In studies covering all segments of distribution, 75% to 100% of vaccine shipments were exposed. More rigorous study designs found higher freeze exposure. **[PRIMARY: abstract]** [PubMed 17382434](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17382434/)
- Hanson CM, George AM, Sawadogo A, Schreiber B., "Is freezing in the vaccine cold chain an ongoing issue? A literature review", Vaccine 2017;35(17):2127–33, doi:10.1016/j.vaccine.2016.09.070. **[PRIMARY: abstract]** [PubMed 28364920](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28364920/)
  - Scope: 45 studies assessed temperature monitoring, 29 of them specifically for "too cold". 41 covered storage, 15 transport and 4 outreach. Sample sizes ranged from 1 to 103 shipments and from 3 to 440 storage units.
  - Below-range exposure during storage was **33% in wealthier countries and 37.1% in lower-income countries**.
  - During shipments, below-range exposure appeared in **38% of studies from higher-income countries and 19.3% from lower-income countries**.
  - The authors highlight health workers' lack of knowledge about freeze damage.
  - Caveat: the abstract does not make the denominator for the storage percentages explicit (pooled share of units or time across studies). Quote it as "about one-third of storage units/observations" rather than as a precise national rate.
- Techathawat et al. 2007 (Vaccine 25(7):1328–33) monitored 48 shipment routes in Thailand's national programme and found extreme cold a bigger problem than heat, with exposure below −0.5 °C widespread. **[PRIMARY: abstract via Europe PMC]** [PubMed 17157419](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17157419/)
- WHO's handbook itself says studies "in developing and industrialized countries throughout the world have shown that accidental exposure to sub-zero temperatures" is common, citing Matthias 2007. **[PRIMARY]** [WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)

**India-specific freeze/heat exposure**
- **Murhekar et al. 2013**, Bull WHO 91(12):906–13, 10 Indian states. Boxes holding electronic recorders and DTP vials were shipped as routine stock. Temperatures exceeded 8 °C for 14.3%, 13.2%, 8.3% and 14.7% of storage time at state, regional and district stores and peripheral health facilities respectively. They fell below 0 °C for 1.5%, 0.2%, 0.6% and **10.5%** of that time. In transit, about **18%** of time was below 0 °C and about 7% above 8 °C. In end-of-study shake tests, **two-thirds of vials showed evidence of freezing**. **[PRIMARY: abstract]** [PubMed 24347729](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24347729/), DOI 10.2471/BLT.13.119974
- **Das et al. 2020**, Trop Dis Travel Med Vaccines 6:8. Bihar, Gujarat and Kerala in 2014–15; 213 devices at 191 facilities; LogTag TRIX-8 loggers reading every 15 min; 59,039.7 device-hours. **[PRIMARY: full text via PMC]** [PMC7268609](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7268609/)
  - Pooled exposure: **below 0 °C for 14.8%** of storage time (below −3 °C: 6.0%) and above 8 °C for 6.6%. Only **60.8% of time was in the 2–8 °C range**. The observed range ran from −33.0 °C to +34.9 °C.
  - **ILRs** were below 0 °C for **16.0%** of time (Bihar 19.5%, Gujarat 15.4%, Kerala 5.1%) and above 8 °C for 5.6%.
  - Sub-district stores (CHC/PHC) were below 0 °C for 17.7% of time.
  - Routine paper logbooks showed deviations in only **5.6% of devices**, despite logger-detected excursions.
  - Frozen freeze-sensitive vials were found at 25 facilities, yet no vials had been discarded for freezing according to store records.
  - Caveat: the paper is internally inconsistent (223 vs 213 devices; state-level above-8 °C figure of 1.3% in the abstract vs 5.2% in Table 2).
- A 2019 study in Bihar, Kerala and Gujarat (21 districts, 110 transport episodes) found that 9–35% of inter-facility transfers and 18% of outreach transfers were exposed to sub-zero temperatures. **[SECONDARY: search excerpt only; not verified in full text]** [Indian Journal of Public Health 2019 via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/2019a3f7845d466d8262d4d6a00f14f3)

### Inferences
- Freezing, not heat, is the dominant hidden failure mode at India's peripheral level (PHC/CHC ILRs). It is invisible to VVMs, which register only cumulative heat, and to paper logs. That makes it a strong "problem" anchor for a predictive system.
- Two independent Indian datasets (Murhekar 2013 and Das 2020) agree that roughly 10–18% of storage time at the peripheral level is below 0 °C. This is a defensible, sourced "India problem" figure.
- Paper records understate the problem. In Das 2020, logbooks flagged only 5.6% of devices even though loggers recorded about 15% of time below 0 °C. That supports the case for continuous digital monitoring plus analytics.

### Gaps
- I did not read the Hanson 2017 full text, so the per-study breakdown and the exact definition of the 33% and 37.1% figures are unverified.
- I found no post-2020 multi-state Indian logger study after eVIN loggers became universal. Whether eVIN-era freeze exposure has fallen is undocumented in the sources I found.

## Q2. Causes of excursions in health-facility refrigerators, with quantitative breakdowns where they exist

### Takeaway
WHO lists the canonical causes:
- **Heat:** power failure, lack of fuel, equipment breakdown, and use of domestic fridges.
- **Freezing:** thermostat mis-adjustment, vaccines placed near evaporators or cold spots, ILR baskets not used, and unconditioned ice-packs.

Field root-cause data are scarce, small-sample and context-specific. Equipment faults and solar batteries dominated in Uganda and Mozambique. Power instability is pervasive: more than 50% of EVM-assessed countries report voltage fluctuations above ±15%. Maintenance, defrosting and thermostat practice drive excursions in Indian ILRs.

### Cited Findings
**WHO's cause taxonomy (WHO/IVB/15.04, Table 3)** **[PRIMARY]** [WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)
- Heat exposure in storage: "Electrical power failures causing breaks in the cold chain"; lack of fuel for gas or kerosene refrigerators; "Cold chain equipment breakdown"; storing vaccines in domestic refrigerators or freezers.
- Freeze exposure in storage: freeze-sensitive vaccines stored near cold-room refrigeration units or other cold spots; "Incorrect thermostat adjustment"; not using the baskets supplied with ILRs; storing near the evaporator plate in domestic refrigerators.
- Transport: too few or wrong coolant packs (heat); unconditioned ice-packs (freezing); trips longer than the passive container's cold life.

**PQS E003 design responses that reveal the known causes** **[PRIMARY]** [WHO PQS E003 catalogue](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e003_0.pdf)
- New prequalified compression refrigerators have **non-adjustable thermostats**. In **2016** PQS introduced freeze-protection grades A/B/C. Grade A is "low vaccine freezing risk even if baskets are not used", intended to "eliminate accidental freezing due to improper user interventions, such as thermostat adjustment or misplacement of vaccines".
- ILRs: during refreezing, "some areas of an ILR can fall below 0 °C". For non-Grade-A models, freeze-sensitive vaccines should never sit within 150 mm of the base.
- Solar battery systems: "Maintenance and replacement of batteries and regulators remains the major problem". Replacement is typically needed after about 5 years.
- Unless mains power is highly reliable, a voltage stabilizer should be ordered for all mains- or generator-powered equipment.

**Quantitative breakdowns**
- **Lennon et al. 2017** (PATH/Nexleaf root-cause analysis, Vaccine 35(17):2198–2202) covered 59 failed refrigerators and freezers in Uganda and 27 refrigerators in Mozambique. **[PRIMARY: abstract]** [PubMed 28364931](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28364931/)
  - Uganda: "the vast majority of failures were due to a cooling unit fault in one widely used refrigerator model".
  - Mozambique: **11 of 27** problems were solar refrigerators whose batteries could not hold a charge, and **8 of 27** needed thermostat adjustment.
  - Context cited: harsh environments, intermittent power, insufficient maintenance capacity and scarce spare parts lead to "high failure rates".
- **Nexleaf remote-monitoring pilot**, reported by the Gates Foundation's Grand Challenges (D. Sarley, 9 Mar 2020): **[SECONDARY-institutional; underlying dataset not published in what I found]** [Gates Foundation Grand Challenges](https://gcgh.grandchallenges.org/article/keeping-vaccines-cold)
  - Paper charts captured "only 10%" of excursions.
  - Night-time power outages "only contributed to a third of the excursions". Flat batteries in solar fridges, "even new ones", contributed "more than two thirds".
- **EVM global data (2009–2020)**: "More than 50% of countries experience significant voltage fluctuations" (above ±15%). **[PRIMARY]** [WHO EVM Global Data Analysis 2009–2020, July 2021 update](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/immunization/supply-chain/evm-global-data-analysis-2009-2020.pdf)
- **India, Das et al. 2020** (Bihar, Gujarat, Kerala, 2014–15), causes identified by the authors **[PRIMARY]** [PMC7268609](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7268609/):
  - lack of routine maintenance, including defrosting
  - thermostat settings not adjusted to ambient temperature
  - irregular power supply and shortage of trained staff
  - door gaskets ill-fitting or damaged in **23.3% of devices**
  - preventive-maintenance documentation missing at **85% of facilities**
  - four PHCs using domestic fridges or cold boxes because of ILR breakdown
- **India, Kumar et al. 2023** (Vaccine: X) evaluated new CCE in India over 15 months in 2016–17 with 31 devices. ILRs had at least one malfunction, "mostly due to the printed circuit board's sensitivity to the erratic power supply". Solar direct-drive units had none. **[PRIMARY: abstract]** [PubMed 37736536](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37736536/)
- **Door openings and defrost (NIST, Chojnacky & Rodriguez 2020, PLoS ONE)**: **[PRIMARY: abstract]** [PubMed 32639973](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32639973/)
  - In domestic refrigerators, water-bottle thermal ballast at 10–15% of volume kept vaccines at 2–8 °C for **4–6 hours without power**.
  - Ballast "does not reliably reduce the frequency or severity of temperature excursions caused by repeated door opening, accidental 'door left open' events, or refrigerator defrost cycle activation."
- **Energy access (WHO/World Bank/IRENA/SEforALL, "Energizing Health", Jan 2023)**: **[PRIMARY: WHO news release and fact sheet]** [WHO news, 14 Jan 2023](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-01-2023-close-to-one-billion-people-globally-are-served-by-health-care-facilities-with-no-electricity-access-or-with-unreliable-electricity); [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/electricity-in-health-care-facilities)
  - Close to **1 billion people** in low- and lower-middle-income countries are served by health facilities with no or unreliable electricity.
  - About **12% (South Asia)** and **15% (sub-Saharan Africa)** of health-care facilities have no electricity access.
  - Only about half of hospitals in sub-Saharan Africa have reliable electricity.
- **EVM, knowledge and SOPs**: fewer than 50% of national stores have vaccine-management SOPs. Most countries lack an up-to-date CCE inventory. **[PRIMARY]** [WHO EVM GDA 2009–2020](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/immunization/supply-chain/evm-global-data-analysis-2009-2020.pdf)

### Inferences
- The causes split into two groups:
  - **Slow-developing, signal-rich failures**: compressor or cooling-unit degradation, battery decline, gasket leaks, ice build-up from missed defrosting, and thermostat drift. These usually show precursors in temperature, duty-cycle and power data that a predictive model could detect.
  - **Abrupt events**: power cuts and door left open. Here value comes from forecasting holdover time and time-to-breach rather than predicting the event itself.
- No source gives a statistically representative, multi-country percentage split of excursion causes (power vs door vs equipment vs maintenance). The best figures come from small root-cause studies, Uganda/Mozambique 2017 and the Nexleaf pilot. That absence is itself a pitch point: data-driven root-cause attribution is a gap.

### Gaps
- I found no published quantitative attribution of excursions to door openings in LMIC health-facility fridges.
- I found no eVIN-derived national statistics on causes of temperature alarms in India (power vs equipment vs handling).
- I did not fully read the Nexleaf ColdTrace Mozambique evaluation (2014–15 poster): uptime 86% with RTM+SMS vs 79% with 30-DTR vs 62% with stem thermometers, and "50% fewer cold excursions". These are preliminary conference-poster figures. **[SECONDARY]** [TechNet-21 Nexleaf poster, 2015](https://www.technet-21.org/images/TC2015/Posters/NexLeaf_Poster.pdf)

## Q3. Measured scale of vaccine wastage and cold-chain failure, globally and in India, including the "50%" figure

### Takeaway
The best measured global evidence is WHO's EVM database: 2009–2020 data from 93 countries, with indicator data from 86 countries and about 5,800 sites.
- **Temperature monitoring (criterion E2) is one of the weakest criteria worldwide**, with medians below the 80% standard at every supply-chain level.
- A third of cold rooms and refrigerators lack recommended monitoring equipment.
- One in 20 stores or facilities lost vaccine to temperature damage in the prior 12 months.

The popular "up to 50% of vaccines are wasted" figure **traces to an unreferenced sentence in a 2005 WHO guideline about total wastage**. Most of that is open-vial wastage of multi-dose vials, not cold-chain failure. **It should not be used as a temperature-excursion statistic.**

### Cited Findings
**WHO EVM Global Data Analysis 2009–2020 (EVM1 database, July 2021 update)** **[PRIMARY]** [PDF](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/immunization/supply-chain/evm-global-data-analysis-2009-2020.pdf); [WHO EVM page](https://www.who.int/teams/immunization-vaccines-and-biologicals/essential-programme-on-immunization/supply-chain/effective-vaccine-management-(evm)/global-data-analysis)
- Scale:
  - A criterion is "Effective" if it scores at least 80%.
  - Data were collected 2009–2020 in **93 countries across all 6 WHO regions**.
  - Indicator data exist for 86 countries: 144 primary stores, 970 sub-national stores, 1,974 lowest-distribution stores and **2,678 immunization service facilities**.
  - A search excerpt of WHO's page describes "178 EVM assessments ... in 89 countries", which conflicts slightly with the "93 countries" in the slide footnotes.
- Criterion results:
  - "The weakest criteria are **E2 [Temperature monitoring]**, E7 and E9 for which the median scores are below 80% at all levels of the supply chain."
  - "Apart from in EUR and AMR, few countries meet the EVM standard for temperature monitoring, at any level."
  - Median E5 (maintenance) scores at sub-national, lowest-distribution and service levels are below 80% in all regions except AMR.
  - Comparing first and last assessments in 64 countries: "No improvement in E2 and E5" (the E2 standard was raised in 2012).
- Indicator results:
  - "**A third of all cold rooms and refrigerators do not have the recommended temperature monitoring equipment.**"
  - "Very few stores or facilities monitor the exposure to freezing temperatures during transport."
  - "Only a quarter of countries have conducted a temperature monitoring study in the past 5 years."
  - "Only about 20% of cold rooms have been temperature mapped."
  - "**One in 20 stores and facilities have lost vaccine in the past 12 months due to temperature damage.**"
  - "More than a half of vaccine storage facilities do not monitor vaccine wastage."
  - "Most countries do not have an up-to-date cold chain equipment inventory."
  - "About 25% and 40% of national and sub-national stores respectively still use paper-based stock management systems."
- EVM2 replaced EVM1 from 2019. WHO now points to an interactive "Global Insights" dashboard instead of the static analysis.

**Origin of the "up to 50% of vaccines wasted" figure: VERDICT, misattributed and out of context**
- The earliest WHO text I found is the **introduction of WHO/V&B/03.18.Rev.1, "Monitoring vaccine wastage at country level: Guidelines for programme managers" (printed May 2005)**: "The World Health Organization reports over 50% vaccine wastage around the world." **No reference, dataset or method is given for this sentence.** **[PRIMARY: PDF read]** [WHO IRIS 10665/68463](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/68463); [PDF](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/bf88e08d-273b-48df-93b7-a1d7cb9ac02a/content)
- The same document defines wastage as **both opened-vial and unopened-vial** losses:
  - It says wastage in opened vials "cannot be eliminated" and is mainly a function of multi-dose vial size and session size.
  - Only "wastage in unopened vials is usually attributable to cold chain and stock management problems".
  - It quotes the GAVI allowance at the time: maximum 25% wastage in year 1, falling to 15% by year 3, for multi-dose vials, and 5% for single- or two-dose vials. This shows that high "wastage" rates were largely expected open-vial losses. **[PRIMARY]** (same PDF)
- The figure was later re-cast as a temperature claim, for example "up to 50 per cent of vaccines are wasted globally every year" in UNEP's 2021 story on optimised cold chains for COVID-19 vaccines, and "more than 50% of vaccines may be wasted globally every year" in The Conversation (2018). Vendor and logistics blogs further attribute it to "lack of temperature control". **[SECONDARY]** [UNEP story](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/story/why-optimized-cold-chains-could-save-billion-covid-vaccines); [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/cracking-the-cold-chain-challenge-is-key-to-making-vaccines-ubiquitous-99329)
- **Recommendation:** do not cite "50% wasted due to cold-chain failure". If used at all, phrase it as: "a 2005 WHO guideline stated, without citation, that total vaccine wastage (including routine open-vial discard of multi-dose vials) exceeded 50% globally." Prefer the EVM "1 in 20 facilities lost vaccine to temperature damage in 12 months" and the freeze-exposure studies.

**Cold-chain equipment (CCE) scale and the Gavi CCEOP**
- Gavi created the Cold Chain Equipment Optimisation Platform (CCEOP) in 2015 and launched it as a funding mechanism in 2016. It followed the 2014 global Immunization Supply Chain Strategy (2015–2020), which prioritised functioning CCE. **[PRIMARY: abstracts]** [Prosser et al. 2022, BMC Health Serv Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36207724/); [Nkwain et al. 2025, Int Health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38333954/)
- The CCEOP evaluation (Guinea, Kenya, Pakistan; 2018–2021) found CCEOP "effective at increasing the number of available and reliable CCE" but identified "gaps in the overall supply chain system, including CCE maintenance". **[PRIMARY: abstract]** [Prosser et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36207724/); [Gavi CCEOP evaluation page](https://www.gavi.org/our-impact/evaluation-studies/cceop-evaluation)
- In Cameroon, CCEOP equipment took 257 days (more than 9 months) from warehouse to facility, plus a median 53 days from installation to commissioning. **[PRIMARY: abstract]** [Nkwain et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38333954/)
- UNICEF Supply Division reported that by end-2019, more than 20,000 CCEOP units had been installed and nearly 20,000 more were in delivery. A Gavi 2018 presentation cited US$250m for 2017–2021 targeting about 62,000 health facilities, while UNICEF described a roughly $400m, 56-country programme. **[PRIMARY-snippet / SECONDARY: conflicting programme-size figures]** [UNICEF Supply story](https://www.unicef.org/supply/stories/keeping-vaccines-safe-through-last-mile-their-journey); [Gavi presentation via ESMAP](https://www.esmap.org/sites/default/files/events-files/Int%20Conf%20Sust%20Cooling/Sushila%20Maharjan%20Gavi_ColdChain_updated_optimized.pdf)

**India-specific scale**
- See Q1 for exposure-time figures (Murhekar 2013; Das 2020).
- Under eVIN, "vaccine utilisation" (doses consumed or issued) in 12 states fell from 305.3 million doses before eVIN to 215.0 million after, which the authors frame as savings of about 90 million doses through reduced over-indenting and wastage. **[PRIMARY: abstract]** [Gurnani et al. 2020, PLoS ONE](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33151951/)

### Inferences
- For a pitch, the strongest globally authoritative lines are WHO's own EVM findings:
  - Temperature monitoring is among the weakest-performing supply-chain functions and did not improve between assessments.
  - A third of fridges and cold rooms lack proper monitoring.
  - 5% of facilities lost stock to temperature damage in a year.
- Measured wastage specifically due to temperature excursions is poorly quantified worldwide. More than half of facilities do not even calculate wastage, which is a data gap a monitoring and prediction system addresses.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative global estimate of the share or value of vaccines lost specifically to temperature excursions.
- I did not retrieve figures from the Gavi CCEOP endline report (Jan 2022) on percent of CCE functional and breakdown rates; only titles of those figures surfaced.
- I found no current (2024–2026) cumulative count of CCEOP units installed.
- EVM2 (2019 onward) global results sit in an interactive dashboard I could not query.

## Q4. India: eVIN, its outcomes, its evolution (CoWIN, U-WIN), and the scale of UIP cold-chain points and refrigerators

### Takeaway
- **eVIN** (MoHFW with UNDP, Gavi HSS-funded) launched in **2015 in 12 states**. By 2020–21 it was nationwide across about **28,500–29,500+ cold chain points (CCPs)** in all 36 states/UTs, with about 24,000–25,000 SIM-enabled temperature loggers.
- Reported outcomes are mainly stock-related:
  - peer-reviewed: a 30.4% reduction in facilities with stock-outs and about 90 million doses saved
  - UNDP-reported: an 80% lower stock-out frequency and more than 99% availability
- **No published source quantifies a reduction in temperature excursions attributable to eVIN.**
- eVIN's stack was extended into **CoWIN** (Jan 2021) and then **U-WIN** (pilot Jan 2023, national launch Oct 2024).
- India's UIP runs about 29,000+ CCPs with tens of thousands of ILRs and deep freezers.

### Cited Findings
**eVIN basics**
- eVIN "digitizes vaccine stocks and monitors the temperature of the cold chain through a smartphone application". It was rolled out in 12 states in 2015. **[PRIMARY: abstract]** [Gurnani et al. 2020, PLoS ONE 15(11):e0241369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33151951/)
- UNDP describes eVIN as an MoHFW initiative supported by UNDP under Gavi Health System Strengthening support. One UNDP page says it was "introduced in 2014", conflicting with the 2015 date used elsewhere. **[PRIMARY-snippet]** [UNDP, "eVIN to Co-WIN"](https://www.undp.org/india/blog/evin-co-win-digitizing-indias-immunization-programme)
- **Loggers:** SIM-enabled temperature loggers with a digital sensor in the refrigerator record temperature **every 10 minutes** and upload **every 60 minutes via GPRS**. Out-of-range readings trigger alarms and SMS/email alerts to cold chain technicians and managers. **[SECONDARY: government portal explainer derived from PIB]** [Vikaspedia (MeitY portal)](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/health/health-care-innovations/health-system-strengthening-1/evin-project-of-health-ministry-health-system-strengthening-1-1?lgn=en)
- A 2016 Lok Sabha written answer said eVIN was running in 11 states: UP, MP, Rajasthan, Odisha, Bihar, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Assam, Manipur, Nagaland and Gujarat. **[PRIMARY-snippet]** [Lok Sabha answer, eparlib](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/694070/1/45796.pdf)

**eVIN scale over time** (figures differ by date and source)
- An intermediate PIB-derived figure: 23,507 CCPs in 585 districts of 22 states and 2 UTs, with about 23,900 loggers. **[SECONDARY]** [Biospectrum](https://biospectrumindia.com/news/22/16985/health-ministry-employs-e-vaccine-intelligence-network.html)
- UNDP Annual Progress Report 2020: eVIN "fully operational in entire India, covering all the 28,522 CCPs across 36 states and UTs"; 24,120 temperature loggers installed; 72% reduction in stock-outs; average stock-out duration down from 5 to 3 days. **[PRIMARY-snippet]** [UNDP APR 2020](https://info.undp.org/docs/pdc/Documents/IND/00098752_AnnuaL%20progress%20Report%20%202020_00101970.pdf)
- UNDP project page: implemented in all 733 districts of 36 states/UTs; nearly 25,000 temperature loggers; more than 29,000 CCPs live; more than 80% reduction in stock-outs; more than 99% vaccine availability. **[PRIMARY-snippet]** [UNDP India eVIN project page](https://www.undp.org/india/projects/improving-vaccination-systems-evin)

## Q5. WHO PQS standards: E003 refrigerators, E006 monitoring devices (30-DTR, RTMD/EMS), and the exact alarm logic

### Takeaway
The alarm values are confirmed from the PQS source documents:
- **Heat alarm:** a single event at +8 °C or above for 10 hours (stationary refrigerators). For mobile appliances it is +10 °C for 10 h; for freezers, −15 °C or above for 60 min.
- **Freeze alarm:** a single event at −0.5 °C or below for 60 minutes.
- **Door alarm:** more than 5 minutes continuously open (more than 30 s for cold rooms).
- **Power alarm:** more than 24 h of continuous no power.

These are threshold-plus-duration rules. Since 2024 all new PQS vaccine refrigerators must carry an Equipment Monitoring System (EMS), and **from 2026 all vaccine refrigerators must**. WHO's own catalogue names "predictive failure responses" as the goal.

### Cited Findings
**E006 30-day electronic refrigerator temperature logger (30-DTR): spec E006/TR06** **[PRIMARY: PDF read]** [WHO/PQS/E006/TR06.3 (issued 8 Sep 2010)](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/PQS_E006_TR06.3.pdf)
- Measure storage temperature at intervals of **≤10 minutes** and log at intervals of **≤1 hour**. Keep 30 days of data and incrementally overwrite older data.
- Alarm settings "must be factory programmed":
  - **Low alarm:** "Exposure to a single temperature event of -0.5 °C or below for 60 minutes."
  - **High alarm:** "Exposure to a single temperature event of +8 °C or above for 10 hours."
- The revision history (26 Jan 2010) shows "4.2.12: High alarm setting changed to +8 °C or above for 10 hours".
- The current version is **E006/TR06.4** (last revised Jan 2022). Its verification protocol E006/TR06.VP.4 (last revised 12 May 2022) still tests "low alarm" and "high alarm" triggers. I did not open TR06.4 itself, but the identical values are confirmed in the 2023 DS01.2 data standard below. [TR06.4 page](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/key-resources/documents/pqs-performance-specification-e006tr064-30-day-electronic-refrigerator); [TR06 VP.4](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/PQS_E006_TR06_VP.4_06_16_2022.pdf)

**E006 Equipment Monitoring Systems: default alarm thresholds in the data standard E006/DS01.2** **[PRIMARY: PDF read]** [WHO/PQS/E006/DS01.2 "Data standards for cold chain equipment monitoring" (issued 10 Jan 2022, revised 30 Nov 2023)](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/PQS%20E006%20DS01.2.pdf)
- **Alarm thresholds (clause 4.2):**
  - Heat (HEAT):
    - stationary refrigeration appliances: "+8 °C or above for 10 hours"
    - mobile refrigeration appliances: "+10 °C or above for 10 hours"
    - freezers: "-15 °C or above for 60 minutes"
  - Freeze (FRZE): refrigeration appliances "-0.5 °C or below for 60 minutes"; no low-temperature alarm required for freezers.
  - Door/lid (DOOR): "Greater than 5 minutes of continuous door or lid opening"; for cold or freezer rooms, "Greater than 30 seconds".
  - Power outage (POWR): "greater than 24 hours of continuous no power" for mains- and solar-powered stationary appliances; no power alarm for mobile appliances.
  - External EMD disconnect (DCNT): more than 30 minutes disconnected from the M2M port.
  - Acknowledged-but-continuing alarms carry the "ACK" suffix (HEATACK, FRZEACK and so on).
- **KPIs (clause 4.3)**, all computed over the past 30 days:
  - heat and freeze alarms: number of alarms, number lasting 48 h or more, cumulative hours, and average duration
  - door: average openings per day, average opening duration, and door alarms
  - power: average hours of power per day, number of power alarms, and cumulative duration
  - "Uptime score" = percentage of time with no active alarm
- **Functional-status rule:** a refrigerator is "non-functional" in a 30-day window if any of these hold:
  - 5 or more heat alarms of over 10 hours
  - 1 or more freeze alarms of over 1 hour
  - 1 or more heat alarms of 48 hours or longer
- All raw data must be downloadable as **JSON** under a standard data-object schema (Annex 1 and 2).

**E006/EM01 Equipment Monitoring Devices (EMD)** **[PRIMARY: PDF read]** [WHO/PQS/E006/EM01.1 (issued 10 Jan 2022)](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/PQS_E006_EM01.1.pdf). EM01.2 is dated 30 Nov 2023 per WHO listing ([EM01.2 page](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/key-resources/documents/pqs-performance-specification-e006em012-equipment-monitoring-devices-0)).
- "Required alarms and their default threshold settings shall comply with the latest published version of the Data Standard specification (WHO/PQS/E006/DS01)."
- Temperature sampling must occur **at least every 15 minutes**. Event data such as door opening are recorded on an event basis.
- Remote EMDs must communicate an alarm **within 15 minutes** of an alarm state. The local audible alarm must reach 70 dB(A) at 1 m.
- The display must show at least the last 30 days of heat and freeze alarm history.

**EMS tiers and mandate** **[PRIMARY]** [WHO PQS E006 catalogue (category text "Latest version: September 2024"; catalogue version date 23 Sep 2026)](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e006_0.pdf); [EMS FAQ v2.0](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/ems-frequently-asked-questions-v2.0.pdf)
- The three levels:
  - Level 1: datalogger with an M2M interface (DL01)
  - Level 2: local EMD (EM01)
  - Level 3: EMD with remote communications, such as GSM/SIM or Wi-Fi
- "All refrigerators must come equipped with an integrated Level 2 EMD, integrated Level 3 EMD or 30-day temperature logger device".
- "**As of 2024, all NEW vaccine refrigerators are required to have an EMS. Starting in 2026, ALL vaccine refrigerators will be required to have an EMS.**"
- Future direction, quoted from the E006 catalogue: monitoring "additional performance parameters of CCE, including voltage input, humidity, compressor function, etc." to enable "**remote failure analysis and ideally predictive failure responses**", which "could ultimately lead to monitoring technology that helps programmes reduce CCE downtime and risk to vaccines."
- 30-DTRs operate for 2 years after activation (sealed batteries), are "NOT suitable for use in vaccine freezers", and are intended for "health facility and lower sub-national levels where routine manual recording is known to be unreliable".

**RTMD / programmable remote temperature and event monitoring systems (E006/TR03.2)** **[PRIMARY: catalogue + handbook]** [E006 catalogue](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e006_0.pdf); [WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)
- These are networks of sensors with central recording and integrated alarms, which "can be internet-enabled which allows for remote monitoring".
- WHO's 2015 handbook guidance: each sensor should record "at least six times per hour", and alarm-event records should be kept for at least three years.
- For newer appliances, the EMS Level 3 EMD effectively becomes the RTMD, using the DS01 default thresholds above.

**Freeze indicators (E006/IN03 irreversible electronic freeze indicators)** **[PRIMARY: product data sheets in catalogue]** [E006 catalogue](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e006_0.pdf)
- Prequalified devices alarm on exposure to −0.5 °C (±0.5 °C) for 60 minutes (±5 min). Examples: the LogTag freeze indicator shows "REJECT" if below −0.5 °C for more than 60 minutes continuously; FreezeAlert uses an "alarm setting of -0.5 °C for a continuous period of 60 minutes".
- Freeze indicators "should be used routinely for internal distribution of freeze-sensitive vaccines".

**E003 refrigerators and freezers (key performance rules)** **[PRIMARY]** [WHO PQS E003 catalogue](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e003_0.pdf)
- Standard mains compression refrigerators: hold +2 to +8 °C with no freezing. On power interruption they must stay below +8 °C for at least **4 hours** at hot-zone ambient.
- ILRs: "can maintain acceptable temperatures below +8 °C on as little as eight hours of electricity per 24 hours". The lining keeps vaccine cool for "at least 20 hours" without power. Newer ILR-like products give 20 to more than 120 hours of autonomy and can work on as little as 28 h of power per week.
- Grade A/B/C freeze-protection grading (2016) and non-adjustable thermostats on new compression units.
- Solar direct-drive (SDD) is preferred over solar-battery systems because of battery failures.
- Specifications include E003/RF01.4 (mains), RF03.6 (intermittent mains, i.e. ILR-type), RF04.4 (solar with battery), RF05.7 (SDD without battery), RF06.4 (SDD with ancillary battery), FZ04.1 (SDD freezer), ULT01.1 (ultra-low freezer) and TS01.1 (transportable powered appliances).
- "A Level 1 Electronic Monitoring Device (EMD) is now required for all vaccine refrigerators. Additionally, an integrate[d] Level 2 EMD or 30-DTR is also required."
- Temperature-zone selection is based on the mean maximum of the hottest month. WHO notes the "hot zone" and the +43 °C test conditions; EM01 uses +43 °C as the hot-zone test ambient.

### Inferences
- **Core pitch insight:** the WHO-standard heat alarm fires only after **10 continuous hours at +8 °C or above**, and the freeze alarm after **60 minutes at −0.5 °C or below**. Current alarms are by design retrospective notifications of an excursion already in progress. A system that forecasts time-to-breach (for example from compressor duty cycle, ambient temperature, power state and door events) gives lead time inside the window the standards leave unalarmed.
- The 2024/2026 EMS mandate and DS01's standardised JSON data objects (15-minute sampling, door, power, compressor and humidity) create a uniform data feed across all new PQS refrigerators worldwide. A model trained on that schema is portable across vendors and countries.
- DS01's "functional status" and "uptime score" KPIs give a ready-made, WHO-defined target variable and evaluation metric for prediction. For example, predict "non-functional next 30 days" or predict a HEAT or FRZE alarm.
- WHO itself writes that EMS should ideally enable "predictive failure responses". ThermoTwin can cite this as standards-body demand.

### Gaps
- I did not open the TR06.4 (2022) PDF itself. Its thresholds are inferred to be unchanged from TR06.3, based on the VP.4 alarm tests and the identical DS01.2 values.
- I did not retrieve TR03.2 (RTMD) default threshold text. It is described as "programmable", so thresholds may be user-set.
- I did not verify the exact current E003 temperature-zone limits (for example "hot zone +43 °C, moderate +32 °C") from the spec documents. Only the +43 °C test condition appears in the documents read.

## Q6. WHO guidance on excursion response, vaccine vial monitors (VVM) and the shake test

### Takeaway
- **VVMs** are heat-only, cumulative time-temperature indicators. They are now fixed to all UNICEF-supplied vaccines and come in four stability classes.
- **The shake test** is WHO's field method to detect freeze damage in aluminium-adjuvanted vaccines. It requires a deliberately frozen control vial, and EVM data show only about 40% of service-level health workers know how to do it.
- **WHO's excursion response** is to label suspect vaccine, quarantine it at +2 to +8 °C, and withhold it until a decision on use or disposal.

### Cited Findings
- **VVM** **[PRIMARY]** [WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04); [E006 catalogue](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e006_0.pdf)
  - The VVM's "main purpose... is to ensure that heat-damaged vaccines are not administered".
  - When the inner square matches or is darker than the outer circle (discard point), the instruction is "DO NOT USE THIS VACCINE. INFORM YOUR SUPERVISOR".
  - Days to discard point by VVM type:

    | VVM type | Days at +37 °C | Days at +25 °C | Time at +5 °C |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | VVM30 (high stability) | 30 | 193 | >4 years |
    | VVM14 | 14 | 90 | >3 years |
    | VVM7 | 7 | 45 | >2 years |
    | VVM2 (least stable) | 2 | n/a | n/a |

  - "VVMs or combined VVM/Threshold indicators are now routinely fixed to all vaccines supplied by UNICEF."
  - The PQS specifications are E006/IN05.4 (VVM) and E006/IN06.1 (combined VVM plus threshold indicator).
- **Shake test definition** **[PRIMARY]** [WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)
  - "A method to determine whether vaccines containing aluminium adjuvant have been damaged by freezing."
  - The suspect vial is shaken alongside a control from the same manufacturer and lot, "frozen overnight at -20 °C and then thawed".
  - "A vaccine with a sedimentation rate that is as fast as or faster than that of the control has been frozen."
  - WHO lists "recognizing damaged vaccines through the use of the shake test" among freeze-prevention and detection measures.
- **Knowledge gap (EVM 2009–2020)**: "The shake test is however not well understood by many, especially by health workers at the service delivery level (40%)." By contrast, about 90% of workers understand temperature sensitivities, storage temperatures and VVM reading. **[PRIMARY]** [WHO EVM GDA 2009–2020](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/immunization/supply-chain/evm-global-data-analysis-2009-2020.pdf)
- **Excursion and emergency response (WHO 2015, chapter 6, contingency plans)** **[PRIMARY]** [WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)
  - Every facility should have a written contingency plan for equipment breakdowns, power failures and transport emergencies, rehearsed "like fire drills".
  - After stabilisation, check whether vaccines were damaged. If damage is suspected, "label the vaccines as damaged or potentially damaged and quarantine them at the appropriate storage temperature (+2 °C to +8 °C) so that they are not distributed until a final decision has been taken."
  - Facilities should record monthly high and low alarm events per piece of equipment. Without alarms, they should count excursion days outside +2–+8 °C (or −15 to −25 °C for frozen storage).
- **Temperature-monitoring studies**: WHO recommends a systematic supply-chain temperature study at least every 5 years (WHO/IVB/05.01 Rev.1 protocol, 2011). Completion is a critical EVM indicator. Only a quarter of countries had done one in the previous 5 years (EVM 2009–2020). **[PRIMARY]** [WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04); [EVM GDA](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/immunization/supply-chain/evm-global-data-analysis-2009-2020.pdf)
- **Cold Chain Monitor cards**: WHO no longer recommends them for in-country use; they are confined to international dry-ice shipments. Stem thermometers should "never be used as the primary temperature monitoring device". **[PRIMARY]** [E006 catalogue](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e006_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- The three WHO detection tools are each after-the-fact. VVM records accumulated heat, the shake test detects freezing that has already happened, and the 30-DTR heat and freeze alarms fire after 10 h and 60 min respectively. None warns before damage. That is the gap a predictive digital twin fills.
- Because VVMs cannot detect freezing and the shake test is poorly known, freeze-damaged vaccine is likely administered in practice. Das 2020 found frozen vials at 25 facilities with zero recorded discards. This is a patient-safety argument (reduced potency means children who are under-protected), not only a cost argument.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve WHO's standalone "shake test" job aid or any WHO decision tree for using vs discarding vaccine after specific excursion magnitudes. WHO generally defers to manufacturer stability data, and WHO/IVB/06.10 "Temperature sensitivity of vaccines" (2006) was not read.
