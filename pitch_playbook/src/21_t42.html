Understand the problem

In plain words

Some fixed devices at known positions (beacons, also called anchors) measure how far away a moving tag is, using ultra-wideband radio time of flight or Bluetooth signal strength. From a few distances you compute the tag's X and Y position. In a perfect room with many beacons this is school geometry. In a real hospital the readings are noisy, some beacons drop out for a while, and walls make the signal arrive late (non-line-of-sight, NLOS), so the measured distance is longer than the true one. The task: estimate position anyway, with fewer beacons than ideal, and report how confident you are.

PDF error to be aware of

The "Suggested Technology" line under this problem (time-series forecasting, anomaly detection, classification, explainable AI) is a copy from the cold-storage problem. For this problem think robust estimation, filtering and sensor fusion. Say so on your slide so the judges see you noticed.

What the PDF requires

  • Estimate target X and Y from range and/or signal-strength measurements, using a reduced number of reference points.
  • Handle noise, missing reference points and intermittent loss; identify or reduce the influence of probable NLOS or biased measurements.
  • Use motion history or temporal filtering where useful.
  • Report position with confidence or uncertainty.
  • Compare accuracy under different infrastructure densities and noise conditions.
  • Dataset suggestion: a virtual room, hospital floor or warehouse with known reference coordinates and simulated trajectories; ideal distance and RSSI plus injected noise, packet loss, NLOS bias and missing anchors; ground-truth coordinates. Hidden test with different paths, noise levels and obstruction patterns.
  • Goal: lowest practical error on the unseen test set.

Our concept: LocusNet

1. Anchor-agnostic learning

The model reads a set of anchor tokens (position, range, signal strength, valid flag), so it accepts any number or placement of anchors. Random anchor dropout during training means the hidden test's different layout is just another draw.

2. Learned trust inside a classical filter

A small model scores each anchor's chance of being NLOS and its bias, and those scores set the noise terms of a Kalman or particle filter with a wall-aware map. We keep the guarantees of filtering and add what learning is good at.

3. Honest uncertainty and a placement advisor

Every fix comes with a calibrated 90% ellipse. The same maths tells a facility where a fourth or fifth beacon helps most, which is the PDF's "reduce installation complexity" in one feature.

Why we win

The best live demo in the set: a moving dot on a hospital floor plan, anchors you can switch off, an ellipse that grows honestly, and classical trilateration failing next to it. The score is a number on a hidden set, so it is objectively comparable, and the PDF's "compare infrastructure densities" becomes our headline chart.

Why we might lose

Teams with signal-processing depth will submit strong filters. Our edge must be the combination (anchor-agnostic, calibrated, advisor) and the demo. Sim-to-real gap will be questioned; answer with domain randomisation and a public-dataset sanity check.

How it works inside

1. Simulator

  • Floor plan. A polygon with wall segments (rooms and corridors of a ward, around 30 m by 20 m). A walkable area is stored as polygons so we can constrain particles and classify zones.
  • Trajectories. Pick random waypoints, route along corridors (shortest path on a walkable graph), speed 0.5 to 1.5 m/s with pauses and turns, sampled 5 to 10 Hz.
  • Ranges. r_i = distance(target, anchor_i) + noise + NLOS bias. Noise standard deviation grows slowly with distance. NLOS is decided geometrically (the segment target to anchor crosses a wall) plus random blockage bursts for people and furniture. Bias is positive, exponential or log-normal, larger with more walls crossed.
  • RSSI. Log-distance path loss with per-wall attenuation (a few dB each) and shadow fading, quantised like a real radio.
  • Loss. Bursty packet loss (two-state Gilbert-Elliott chain) plus whole-anchor outages for several seconds.
  • Output table. trajectory id, time, ground-truth x and y, one row per anchor with anchor id and coordinates, range, RSSI, valid flag. Keep the LOS flag in a separate file for analysis only, never as a model input.
  • Hidden split. Different floor layouts, anchor counts and placements, noise levels, NLOS rates and trajectory styles.

3. Robust estimation

  • Weighted least squares. Minimise the sum over anchors of w_i (r_i minus ||x minus a_i||)². Weights from the trust model: w_i = (1 minus P_nlos) divided by sigma_i². Gauss-Newton from a coarse grid initialisation to avoid local minima.
  • Robust loss. Replace the square with a Huber or Cauchy loss so one bad range cannot dominate.
  • Subset consensus (RANSAC style). With K anchors, solve on every subset of size 3 (K is small, so enumeration is cheap), count anchors consistent within a gate, keep the best consensus, refit on its inliers.
  • Two anchors left. Two candidate points. Use the previous position, velocity and the walkable-area map to pick one, and inflate the covariance.
  • One anchor left. Dead reckoning from the motion model; the ellipse grows with time. State this honestly in the UI.

4. Tracking

State x = [px, py, vx, vy]       Motion: constant velocity + process noise Q
Measurement i: z_i = ||p - a_i|| + b_i + n_i
   n_i ~ N(0, sigma_i^2 * (1 + k * P_nlos_i / (1 - P_nlos_i + eps)))      learned trust inflates variance
   b_i  = learned bias estimate (subtracted when P_nlos is high)
Gate: accept measurement i only if Mahalanobis innovation^2 < chi2(1, 0.99)
Particle filter variant: 1000 particles; weight = product of range likelihoods; zero weight if a particle crosses a wall
Output: mean (x, y) and covariance Sigma (from EKF or particle spread)

The particle filter is the answer to the two-anchor mirror ambiguity: both candidates survive as clusters until motion and walls eliminate one.

5. The learned model

  • Input. A set of anchor tokens [ax, ay, r, rssi, valid, implied range, residual] plus the previous estimate. Anchors are unordered, so use attention without positional encodings.
  • Output. A position correction relative to the robust estimate (residual learning is more stable than predicting from scratch) and a covariance through a heteroscedastic Gaussian likelihood (predict log standard deviations and a correlation).
  • Training tricks. Random anchor dropout (0 to 60%), random NLOS rate, noise scale, wall layouts and anchor placements for domain randomisation; curriculum from easy to hard.
  • Temporal part. A small GRU over successive corrected estimates as a smoother.
  • Comparison. Report three systems: classical only, hybrid (learned trust plus filter), end-to-end network. Pick the best on validation; show all in the ablation.

6. Calibrated uncertainty

Compute the Mahalanobis distance of the true error under each predicted covariance on a calibration set. Take the 90th percentile of those scores as a scale factor, so the final ellipse is the predicted ellipse multiplied by that factor. On the test set, report coverage per condition (target 90%). When trace(Sigma) exceeds a threshold, return a zone ("probably Ward B, 78%") by integrating the posterior over room polygons instead of a misleading point.

7. Anchor Placement Advisor

Fisher information at position x:   J(x) = sum_i  (1 / sigma_i(x)^2) * u_i u_i^T ,   u_i = (x - a_i) / ||x - a_i||
Error floor:  CRLB(x) = sqrt( trace( J(x)^-1 ) )
sigma_i(x) is inflated where the line to anchor i crosses walls (NLOS-aware)
Objective: choose K anchor positions from a candidate grid to minimise mean or P90 of CRLB over walkable cells
Method: greedy forward selection (add the anchor that lowers the objective most), then local swap refinement
Report: predicted error heat-map, recommended positions, minimum K for P90 under 1.5 m

Validate the advisor with the simulator: placements it recommends must achieve lower measured error than random or corner placements.

Pitch kit

30-second pitch

Hospitals lose time hunting for wheelchairs and infusion pumps, and indoor positioning is too expensive to cover every room. LocusNet locates a tag from as few as three beacons, spots signals bent by walls, keeps working when beacons drop out, and tells you how sure it is. It also tells the hospital where to place the next beacon, so accuracy costs fewer devices.

5-minute demo script

  1. 0:00 Problem. Floor plan, five beacons, a tag walking through wards. Say "most of the time this works; here is when it does not".
  2. 0:45 Classical trilateration. Switch on an NLOS burst behind a wall; the classical dot jumps through the wall.
  3. 1:30 LocusNet. Same measurements: the dot holds; anchor chip turns red with P(NLOS) 0.8; the ellipse widens slightly.
  4. 2:15 Kill an anchor. Disable two anchors. Ellipse grows, still correct. Disable a third: it says "probably Ward B, 78%" instead of guessing.
  5. 3:00 Experiments. Degradation heat-map and error CDF, with CRLB overlay.
  6. 3:45 Placement advisor. "With four beacons, here is the best layout; predicted P90 1.3 m." Drag one away and show error rising.
  7. 4:30 Close. API call, model size and latency, limitations.

Likely judge questions

Why not just train a neural network end to end?

We did, as one of three systems. The hybrid is more robust, explainable and gives guaranteed behaviour with few anchors. The numbers decide which one ships.

How do you handle only two anchors?

The particle filter keeps both mirror solutions, the map and motion model remove one, and the ellipse is inflated honestly. With one anchor we report a zone.

Is your uncertainty real or decorative?

We calibrate with conformal scaling and report empirical coverage for every condition group; the target is 90% coverage for the 90% ellipse.

How do you know it will work on real hardware?

We randomise noise, bias and layouts, and test on a public dataset; real UWB needs antenna delay calibration and an on-site trial, which is our next step.

What is the placement advisor based on?

Fisher information and the Cramér-Rao bound with wall-aware noise, then greedy selection. We validate it by simulating its layouts against random ones.

Can it track several tags?

Yes; each tag has its own filter. Anchors are shared.

What is the compute cost?

Under 10 ms per update on a laptop CPU; small enough for an edge gateway.

What if an anchor position is wrong?

Anchor survey errors bias everything. We mention it as a limit and propose self-calibration from tag motion as future work.
