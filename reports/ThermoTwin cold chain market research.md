# ThermoTwin Can Warn Before Standard Alarms Fire

ThermoTwin targets a real, well-documented gap that no commercial product fills. The alarms that govern vaccine refrigerators worldwide are designed to fire after an excursion is well under way, and this review found no product that publicly forecasts stationary-storage excursions or explains their causes. Under WHO's prequalification defaults, the heat alarm fires only after a **single continuous event at or above +8 °C lasting 10 hours**, and the freeze alarm only after **60 minutes at or below −0.5 °C** ([WHO PQS E006/DS01.2](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/PQS%20E006%20DS01.2.pdf)). India's measured problem is mostly silent freezing. Two multi-state logger studies found vaccine storage below 0 °C for **10.5–14.8% of the time**. One found that two-thirds of shake-tested DTP vials showed signs of freezing. The other found that paper logbooks flagged deviations at only 5.6% of devices ([Murhekar 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24347729/); [Das 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7268609/)). Globally, WHO's assessments rank temperature monitoring among the weakest vaccine-management functions, and **one in 20 stores and facilities lost vaccine to temperature damage within a year** ([WHO EVM 2009–2020](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/immunization/supply-chain/evm-global-data-analysis-2009-2020.pdf)). WHO makes equipment monitoring mandatory on all vaccine refrigerators from 2026 and names predictive failure response as the long-term goal ([WHO PQS E006 catalogue](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e006_0.pdf)). Commercial "predictive" offerings sit in shipment visibility and in supermarket or laboratory predictive maintenance, on horizons of days to weeks. The closest stationary analogue, Emanate PowerPath (2017), is of unknown status, and large incumbents are consolidating fast. The literature supports a specific stack: a grey-box RC thermal twin fitted to each unit, a gradient-boosted residual or hazard model, calibrated probabilities, and event-level evaluation against WHO-grade events. LLMs should be limited to explanation, translation and drafting, because agent benchmarks show exact root-cause accuracy of 11–21%. Two popular numbers should leave the pitch. "50% of vaccines wasted" comes from an unreferenced 2005 sentence about total wastage, most of it open-vial discard. The "$35 billion" biopharma loss figure has no IQVIA source that could be located.

## Freezing, not heat, dominates India's measured cold-chain failures

WHO requires vaccines in cold rooms and refrigerators to stay between **+2 °C and +8 °C**, and frozen stock between −25 °C and −15 °C ([WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)). The bottom of that band is the harder half. WHO lists pentavalent DTwP-HepB-Hib, hexavalent, hepatitis B, HPV, pneumococcal conjugate, TT/Td and IPV among vaccines damaged by freezing. Yet the vaccine vial monitor (VVM) on every UNICEF-supplied vial records only cumulative heat ([WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)), so freeze damage leaves no visible mark. Accidental freezing is a well-replicated global finding. The first systematic review found 14–35% of refrigerators or shipments exposed vaccine to freezing, and 75–100% of shipments in studies covering whole distribution chains; more rigorous study designs found more freezing ([Matthias 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17382434/)). A 2017 review of 45 studies found below-range exposure in storage of **33% in wealthier and 37.1% in lower-income countries**, and highlighted health workers' poor knowledge of freeze damage ([Hanson 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28364920/)). Its abstract does not define the denominator, so the defensible phrasing is "about one-third of storage observations".

Two independent Indian logger studies converge. Murhekar et al. shipped electronic recorders with routine DTP stock through 10 states. At peripheral health facilities, temperatures were below 0 °C for **10.5%** of storage time and above 8 °C for 14.7%, and about 18% of transit time was below 0 °C. In end-of-study shake tests, **two-thirds of vials showed evidence of freezing** ([Murhekar 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24347729/)). Das et al. logged 213 devices at 191 facilities in Bihar, Gujarat and Kerala in 2014–15, about 59,000 device-hours at 15-minute intervals. They found **14.8%** of storage time below 0 °C and 6.6% above 8 °C, so only 60.8% of time was in range. ILRs were below 0 °C for 16.0% of the time, ranging from 19.5% in Bihar to 5.1% in Kerala. Routine logbooks showed deviations at only **5.6% of devices**. Frozen freeze-sensitive vials turned up at 25 facilities whose records showed no vial discarded for freezing ([Das 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7268609/)). The Das paper contains internal inconsistencies in device counts and state-level heat figures, but its freeze results are consistent. A Nexleaf pilot reported by the Gates Foundation adds that paper charts captured only about 10% of excursions, though the underlying dataset is unpublished ([Gates Foundation Grand Challenges](https://gcgh.grandchallenges.org/article/keeping-vaccines-cold)).

The causes are mundane, and they split into two regimes that call for different kinds of prediction. WHO's taxonomy blames heat exposure on power failures, fuel shortages, equipment breakdown and domestic refrigerators. It blames freezing on incorrect thermostat adjustment, vaccine placed near evaporators or cold spots, and unused ILR baskets ([WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)). Field root-cause studies are small but telling. In Uganda, most of 59 failures traced to a cooling-unit fault in one widely used model. In Mozambique, **11 of 27** problems were solar batteries that could not hold charge and **8 of 27** needed thermostat adjustment ([Lennon 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28364931/)). The Gates-reported Nexleaf pilot found that night-time power cuts explained only a third of excursions, while flat solar batteries, even new ones, contributed more than two-thirds ([Gates Foundation Grand Challenges](https://gcgh.grandchallenges.org/article/keeping-vaccines-cold)). In India, a 15-month evaluation of new equipment found that every ILR had at least one malfunction, mostly because the printed circuit board was sensitive to erratic power; solar direct-drive units had none ([Kumar 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37736536/)). Das et al. found damaged door gaskets on 23.3% of devices and no preventive-maintenance records at 85% of facilities ([Das 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7268609/)).

Power is a structural problem. More than half of EVM-assessed countries report voltage swings beyond ±15% ([WHO EVM](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/immunization/supply-chain/evm-global-data-analysis-2009-2020.pdf)). About **12% of South Asian health facilities have no electricity**, and close to a billion people are served by facilities with no or unreliable power ([WHO, Jan 2023](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-01-2023-close-to-one-billion-people-globally-are-served-by-health-care-facilities-with-no-electricity-access-or-with-unreliable-electricity)). NIST showed that water-bottle ballast keeps a domestic fridge at 2–8 °C for 4–6 hours without power. However, ballast does not reliably prevent excursions from repeated door openings, doors left open or defrost cycles ([Chojnacky & Rodriguez 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32639973/)). The analytic consequence is that slow failures leave precursors in duty-cycle and temperature data days ahead. These include cooling-unit degradation, battery decline, gasket leaks, ice build-up and thermostat drift. Abrupt events such as a power cut or a door left open cannot be predicted before onset, but their time-to-breach can be forecast once they start. No source gives a representative split of excursions by cause.

WHO's Effective Vaccine Management (EVM) database gives the system-level picture. It covers 2009–2020 assessments in 93 countries, with indicator data from roughly 5,800 stores and facilities ([WHO EVM](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/immunization/supply-chain/evm-global-data-analysis-2009-2020.pdf)). Temperature monitoring (criterion E2) is among the three weakest criteria. Its median score falls below the 80% standard at every level of the supply chain, with no improvement between first and last assessments in 64 countries. **A third of cold rooms and refrigerators lack the recommended monitoring equipment.** Only about 20% of cold rooms have been temperature-mapped, and more than half of facilities do not monitor wastage. **One in 20 stores and facilities lost vaccine to temperature damage in the previous 12 months.** That last figure is the most defensible global loss statistic available. The newer EVM2 results sit in a dashboard that could not be queried.

India already has the sensing layer that most LMICs lack. The electronic Vaccine Intelligence Network (eVIN) is run by MoHFW with UNDP under Gavi support. It launched in 12 states in 2015 ([Gurnani 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33151951/)), though one UNDP page says 2014. By 2020 it covered **28,522 cold-chain points across all 36 states and UTs, with 24,120 SIM-enabled loggers** ([UNDP Annual Progress Report 2020](https://info.undp.org/docs/pdc/Documents/IND/00098752_AnnuaL%20progress%20Report%20%202020_00101970.pdf)). Later UNDP pages cite more than 29,000 points and nearly 25,000 loggers ([UNDP eVIN project](https://www.undp.org/india/projects/improving-vaccination-systems-evin)). Each logger records every 10 minutes, uploads hourly over GPRS, and sends SMS and email on out-of-range readings ([Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/health/health-care-innovations/health-system-strengthening-1/evin-project-of-health-ministry-health-system-strengthening-1-1?lgn=en)). The equipment base underneath is known only from secondary reports. A December 2020 ministry briefing was reported as citing 28,947 cold-chain points and 85,634 pieces of equipment ([Orissa Post](https://www.orissapost.com/enough-cold-storage-facilities-available-in-india-for-storage-of-covid-19-vaccine-centre/amp/)), and a 2025 study counts about 1,06,964 ILRs and deep freezers ([IJCH 2025](https://www.iapsmupuk.org/journal/index.php/IJCH/article/download/3406/1719/20158)).

eVIN's published outcomes concern stock, not temperature. A peer-reviewed pre/post study in 12 states found a **30.4% fall in facilities with any stock-out** and about 90 million doses saved ([Gurnani 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33151951/)). UNDP's reported 72–80% reduction measures stock-out frequency, a different metric. **No published source quantifies a reduction in temperature excursions attributable to eVIN.** An independent case study costed eVIN at about US$65 million over six years, with software only 5.5% ([APCTT case study](https://apctt.org/sites/default/files/attachment/2022-02/India_eVIN_CoWIN_case_study.pdf)). U-WIN launched nationally in October 2024 ([Onmanorama](https://onmanorama.com/lifestyle/health/2024/10/29/pm-modi-u-win-portal-registration-log-in-benefits.html)) and is interoperable with eVIN and ABDM ([UNDP](https://www.undp.org/india/blog/giving-digital-boost-universal-immunisation)), which shows the ministry building layered digital public infrastructure. The opening for ThermoTwin is an analytics layer on existing telemetry, not new loggers.

Outside immunisation, the best-sourced loss figures are for food. UNEP and FAO estimate that lack of refrigeration caused the loss of **12% of total food production in 2017**, and that the food cold chain produces 4% of global greenhouse-gas emissions ([UNEP-FAO 2022](https://www.unep.org/resources/report/sustainable-food-cold-chains-opportunities-challenges-and-way-forward)). Press coverage gives the tonnage as 526 million tonnes ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/food/sustainable-cold-chains-can-address-climate-food-crises-unep-fao-report-85966)). The IIR separately estimates the share at over 13% ([IIR 2020](https://iifiir.org/en/news/the-iir-publishes-a-new-informatory-note-on-the-role-of-refrigeration-in-worldwide-nutrition)). Both are modelled estimates. No credible primary estimate of non-vaccine pharmaceutical value lost to excursions was found.

### Five popular statistics fail a source check

Several numbers common in cold-chain pitches do not survive tracing.

The claim that "up to 50% of vaccines are wasted" goes back to a single unreferenced sentence in a 2005 WHO guideline for programme managers. The sentence says WHO reports over 50% vaccine wastage worldwide, with no data or method ([WHO/V&B/03.18.Rev.1](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/68463)). The same guideline defines wastage to include routine discard from opened multi-dose vials, which it says cannot be eliminated. It attributes only unopened-vial losses to cold-chain and stock problems. UNEP and The Conversation later recast the figure as a temperature claim ([UNEP 2021](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/story/why-optimized-cold-chains-could-save-billion-covid-vaccines); [The Conversation 2018](https://theconversation.com/cracking-the-cold-chain-challenge-is-key-to-making-vaccines-ubiquitous-99329)).

The "$35 billion a year lost by biopharma to temperature-control failures" is usually attributed to IQVIA ([Manufacturing Chemist](https://manufacturingchemist.com/ensuring-temperature-sensitive-goods-reach-their-destination-unspoiled)). No IQVIA publication containing it could be found, and trade press traces it to a 2019 survey by Peli BioThermal, a packaging vendor ([Air Cargo News](https://www.aircargonews.net/?p=1023281)).

The figure that 56% of LMIC cold-chain equipment is poorly functioning or non-functional is a WHO/UNICEF statement known only second-hand, through an Intel/Nexleaf case study ([Intel/Nexleaf](https://www.intel.ca/content/dam/www/central-libraries/us/en/documents/nexleaf-analytics-case-study.pdf)).

Nexleaf's statement that 75% of freeze-sensitive vaccines in Murhekar's Indian study showed freeze damage ([Nexleaf RCT report](https://www.nexleaf.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Nexleaf-RTM-RCT-Report.pdf)) overstates the primary abstract, which reports two-thirds of vials ([Murhekar 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24347729/)).

Analyst market sizes are paid-report press releases with undisclosed methods; they are covered in the market section below.

## WHO and CDC alarm rules start the clock after damage begins

WHO's Performance, Quality and Safety (PQS) system specifies both the refrigerators and the monitors. The E003 refrigerator standard rules out domestic refrigerators for vaccine and accepts no freezing (0 °C or below) in the vaccine compartment of compression units. It requires standard mains refrigerators to stay below +8 °C for at least **4 hours** after power loss at hot-zone ambient. ILRs must hold temperature on as little as 8 hours of electricity a day, and keep vaccine cool for at least 20 hours without power ([WHO PQS E003](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e003_0.pdf)). Since 2016, PQS has graded freeze protection A, B or C. Grade A is meant to prevent freezing even when users misadjust thermostats or misplace vaccine, and new compression units have non-adjustable thermostats. In non-Grade-A ILRs, freeze-sensitive vaccine must sit at least 150 mm above the base, because parts of the cabinet fall below 0 °C during refreezing ([WHO PQS E003](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e003_0.pdf)). Holdover is defined as the time during which all points in the compartment stay between +2 °C and +10 °C at the rated maximum ambient after power is cut ([WHO PQS E003/RF03.2](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/PQS_Performance_E003_RF03.2.pdf)). One listed solar direct-drive unit reports about 94 hours at +43 °C ([WHO PQS E003-059](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/immunization_devices/pdf/e003/E003-059e.pdf)). These specified holdover figures are natural priors for ThermoTwin's time-to-breach forecasts during outages.

The monitoring standard, E006, fixes the alarm logic that any forecast must beat. The 30-day temperature recorder (30-DTR) specification requires measurement every 10 minutes or less and logging every hour or less. It also requires ±0.5 °C accuracy from −20 to +20 °C, 0.2 °C resolution and calibration traceable to an ISO/IEC 17025 laboratory. Its alarms are factory-programmed: one for a single event at or below −0.5 °C lasting 60 minutes, and one for a single event at or above +8 °C lasting 10 hours ([WHO PQS E006/TR06.3](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/PQS_E006_TR06.3.pdf)). The current revision, TR06.4 (2022), was not read. Identical values appear in the 2023 data standard DS01.2, which sets the default alarms for all equipment monitoring systems (EMS):

| Alarm (DS01.2 code) | Default trigger | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Heat (HEAT) | ≥ +8 °C for 10 h | Stationary refrigerators |
| Heat (HEAT) | ≥ +10 °C for 10 h | Mobile refrigerators |
| Heat (HEAT) | ≥ −15 °C for 60 min | Freezers |
| Freeze (FRZE) | ≤ −0.5 °C for 60 min | Refrigerators (none for freezers) |
| Door (DOOR) | > 5 min continuously open (> 30 s for cold or freezer rooms) | Units with door sensing |
| Power (POWR) | > 24 h with no power | Mains and solar stationary units |
| Disconnect (DCNT) | External monitoring device disconnected > 30 min | All |

Source: [WHO PQS E006/DS01.2](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/PQS%20E006%20DS01.2.pdf).

DS01.2 also defines 30-day indicators: alarm counts, alarms lasting 48 hours or more, cumulative alarm hours, door openings per day and hours of power per day. It defines an uptime score as the share of time with no active alarm. A refrigerator counts as **non-functional** in a 30-day window if it has five or more heat alarms lasting over 10 hours, any freeze alarm lasting over 1 hour, or any heat alarm of 48 hours or longer. Raw data must be downloadable as JSON in a standard schema ([WHO PQS E006/DS01.2](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/PQS%20E006%20DS01.2.pdf)). The device specification EM01 requires temperature sampling at least every 15 minutes, remote transmission of an alarm within 15 minutes, a 70 dB(A) local alarm, and 30 days of on-screen alarm history ([WHO PQS E006/EM01.1](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/PQS_E006_EM01.1.pdf)).

The regulatory direction points straight at ThermoTwin. WHO's EMS guidance says all new vaccine refrigerators have needed an EMS since 2024, and all vaccine refrigerators will need one from 2026 ([WHO EMS FAQ](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/ems-frequently-asked-questions-v2.0.pdf)). The E006 catalogue describes the next step as monitoring voltage, humidity and compressor function, to enable "remote failure analysis and ideally predictive failure responses" ([WHO PQS E006 catalogue](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e006_0.pdf)). Several PQS product listings showed validity dates of 31 May 2026, which has now passed. Their prequalification status needs rechecking before any partner device is named.

Older remote temperature monitoring devices (RTMDs, spec E006/TR03.2) have user-programmable thresholds. WHO's 2015 handbook asks for at least six readings per hour per sensor and three years of alarm records ([WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)). For India, a city-level eVIN document lists alert thresholds of above +8 °C or below +2 °C for ILRs, and above −15 °C for deep freezers, with no duration rules stated ([Ahmedabad Smart City eVIN document](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/e-vin_health_by_ahmedabad_smartcity_.pdf)). The national logger configuration could not be verified. Prequalified electronic freeze indicators trip at −0.5 °C for 60 minutes, and WHO recommends them routinely when distributing freeze-sensitive vaccine ([WHO PQS E006 catalogue](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/media_document/e006_0.pdf)).

Every downstream detection tool is retrospective. VVMs change colour with cumulative heat and reach their discard point after 30, 14, 7 or 2 days at +37 °C, depending on category ([WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04); [DCVMN](https://dcvmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/choice-of-vvm-category.pdf)). The shake test detects freeze damage in aluminium-adjuvanted vaccines by comparing sedimentation against a control vial frozen overnight. EVM assessments found only about **40% of service-level health workers** understand it, against roughly 90% who can read a VVM ([WHO EVM](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/immunization/supply-chain/evm-global-data-analysis-2009-2020.pdf)). WHO's response protocol is to label suspect stock, quarantine it at +2 to +8 °C and withhold it until a decision is made ([WHO/IVB/15.04](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/WHO-IVB-15.04)).

CDC's Vaccine Storage and Handling Toolkit (July 2026 edition) takes a parallel approach for US providers. Every storage unit needs a digital data logger plus a backup. The logger must have an out-of-range alarm, ±0.5 °C uncertainty and logging at least every 30 minutes. Its probe sits in glycol, glass beads, sand or Teflon in the centre of the unit, and the display sits outside so the door stays shut ([CDC Toolkit 2026](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/downloads/storage-handling-toolkit.pdf)). Staff check min/max temperatures each workday. On an excursion they label the vaccine as not to be used but do not discard it. They then record temperatures, duration, affected lots and how long it took to act, and contact the programme or manufacturer. A repeatedly sounding alarm must not be disconnected until its cause is fixed ([CDC Toolkit 2026](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/downloads/storage-handling-toolkit.pdf)). A CDC-associated simulation found that twice-daily current-temperature readings without min/max caught only **4.8–6.4% of excursions** ([CDC Stacks](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/119983/cdc_119983_DS1.pdf)). That is the clearest quantitative case for continuous monitoring.

For non-vaccine medicines, USP general chapter <1079.2> governs excursion evaluation through mean kinetic temperature (MKT). MKT is an Arrhenius-weighted average of absolute temperatures with a default activation energy ΔH of 83.144 kJ/mol, so that ΔH/R equals 10,000 K. It is always at or above the arithmetic mean ([USP text via mirror](https://trungtamthuoc.com/usp-en/mean-kinetic-temperature-in-the-evaluation-of-temperature-excursions-during-storage-and-transportation-of-drug-products); [Vaisala](https://www.vaisala.com/en/blog/2020-02/common-questions-mkt-answered-trending-interpreting-reports-default-activation-energy)). For controlled cold temperature products (2–8 °C), excursions between 8 and 15 °C are acceptable for at most 24 hours. The condition is that MKT over the 24 hours counted from the excursion stays at or below 8 °C. For controlled room temperature, the limits are excursions of 15–30 °C and MKT at or below 25 °C over 30 days. USP stresses that each excursion is a separate, irreversible event, and that MKT must not be used to excuse a storage system out of control ([USP text via mirror](https://trungtamthuoc.com/usp-en/mean-kinetic-temperature-in-the-evaluation-of-temperature-excursions-during-storage-and-transportation-of-drug-products)). WHO's PQS day/night refrigerator test uses the same default activation energy to require MKT within +2 to +8 °C ([WHO PQS E003/RF03.2](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/PQS_Performance_E003_RF03.2.pdf)). The USP values here come from an unofficial mirror and should be checked against the official compendium.

Together, the standards leave a precise window with no alarm. Under prequalified 30-DTR and EMS defaults, a refrigerator can sit above +8 °C for almost 10 hours, or below −0.5 °C for almost an hour, before an alarm fires. WHO treats that hour of freezing as potentially destructive ([Nexleaf IoT series 2017](https://www.nexleaf.org/wp-content/uploads/reports/IoT-Series_ColdTrace-PDF-Report_2017.pdf)), and VVMs never record it. Forecasting time-to-WHO-alarm, with freezing prioritised, is the job the standards leave undone. DS01.2's HEAT and FRZE alarms and its non-functional rule supply ready-made, vendor-neutral prediction targets, and its JSON schema supplies a portable data interface.

The resulting white space is specific. It combines short-horizon excursion forecasting, automated cause attribution and measurable false-alarm control. These must be delivered on low-cost, low-connectivity RTMD-class hardware, as a vendor-agnostic layer. No vendor found publishes forecast lead time, precision or false-alarm rates. The window is closing. Sensitech bought Berlinger's monitoring business in August 2024, explicitly to enter stationary and global-health monitoring ([Sensitech](https://www.sensitech.com/en/resources/news/news-article/2024-08-02-berlinger-acquisition-completion.html)). Nexleaf holds the largest fridge-telemetry dataset from low- and middle-income countries and has door sensors in deployed hardware. That makes it both the most likely partner and the most likely fast follower.

This gap analysis rests on public materials only, with no customer interviews. ## Physics-anchored hybrids suit sub-hour excursion forecasting

### A per-unit RC twin gives forecasts and causes at once

The best-documented grey-box model of a real refrigeration unit comes from DTU. Sossan et al. fitted first- to fourth-order resistor-capacitor (RC) networks to a 333-litre Bosch freezer, using maximum likelihood with a Kalman-filter likelihood ([Sossan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.06855)). A first-order, air-only model left strongly correlated residuals. A third-order model (heat exchanger, air and envelope) cut the 20-minute-ahead residual standard deviation to **0.29 °C**, against 0.73 °C for second order. A fourth order added nothing significant (likelihood-ratio p = 0.61). The slowest time constant was about **4 hours**, and inside model-predictive control the linear third-order model behaved better than a more accurate nonlinear variant. Choosing model order by forward likelihood-ratio testing follows the building-physics method of Bacher and Madsen ([Bacher & Madsen 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enbuild.2011.02.005)).

Cold-storage studies use simpler forms that still fit well. First-order-plus-delay models of an agricultural cold store held errors below 0.11 °C ([SciELO](https://scielo.br/j/rca/a/tF9s7krYB8Y8bL8Pc4qcbXK/?lang=en)). A UK chilled-room model predicted food temperatures within 0.7 °C ([FRPERC](https://grimsby.ac.uk/documents/frperc/projects/coldroom.pdf)). The physics shows why air spikes mislead. The DTU freezer's interior air had a capacity of about 5×10² J/K, against about 10⁴ J/K for the heat exchanger. So the air responds in minutes, while a loaded product lags by hours.

Refrigeration digital twins remain early. An online-updated compressor model improved cascade-system prediction by 7.42–23.62% over a simplified model ([XJTU](https://faculty.xjtu.edu.cn/chuangwang/zh_CN/article/510574/content/30011.htm)). An AI twin presented at IIR in 2024 is described by its authors as early-stage ([IIR FRIDOC 148877](https://iifiir.org/fr/fridoc/construire-des-jumeaux-numeriques-pour-les-systemes-frigorifiques-a-148877)). No peer-reviewed paper publishes identified RC parameters for a WHO-PQS vaccine refrigerator or a walk-in cold room, so ThermoTwin must identify them for each unit. The payoff is that tracked parameters double as diagnosis. Rising envelope conductance points to a failing gasket. Falling effective COP points to refrigerant loss, condenser fouling or compressor wear. A jump in infiltration conductance points to a door left ajar.

### Fault signatures live in duty cycle, pull-down and warm-up rates

Refrigeration fault detection relies on a handful of physical features. Patents describe refrigerant leaks as lengthening compressor runs, and note that duty cycle varies by hour and season, so baselines must be context-aware ([US12066481B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12066481)). A gradual rise in compressor on-time at constant room temperature signals refrigerant loss or evaporator icing ([US7423546](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7423546)). A decision tree based on COP separates condenser fouling, evaporator frosting, refrigerant leakage and non-condensable gas in single-door appliances ([Senger et al., IIR](https://iifiir.org/fr/fridoc/arbre-de-decision-base-sur-le-coefficient-de-performance-pour-la-149347)).

Classifiers on the ASHRAE RP-1043 chiller dataset report accuracy as high as **99.98%** ([MDPI Actuators](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0825/12/6/242/htm)). Follow-up work shows they struggle with low-severity faults and with shifts between operating conditions ([arXiv:2007.03167](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.03167)), so lab accuracy is not field accuracy. The best open fault datasets are ORNL's display-case data and a new synthetic set. The ORNL set covers six faults at three severities each, including door-open time and evaporator icing, logged every second ([Sun et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8159987/)). The synthetic set, from September 2026, covers 27 refrigerators with 7.07 million one-minute rows and six progressive degradation types, and includes the generator code ([Benamirouche et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22229)). The table below combines those sources with engineering inference; the thermostat and sensor rows are inference.

| Likely cause | Signature in cheap signals | Predictable before breach? |
|---|---|---|
| Refrigerant leak or compressor wear | Longer runs and higher duty cycle at the same ambient; slower pull-down; falling effective COP in the twin | Yes, days to weeks |
| Gasket leak | Faster warm-up while the compressor is off; rising effective UA | Yes, days |
| Door left ajar | Door-sensor duration; sudden jump in the infiltration term | Only after onset; time-to-breach can be forecast |
| Evaporator icing or missed defrost | Pull-down slowly worsens, then resets after defrost | Yes, days |
| Condenser fouling | Duty cycle rising disproportionately with ambient | Yes, days to weeks |
| Power loss | Zero compressor current; mains detector off | Event no; time-to-breach yes, from holdover |
| Thermostat drift (freeze risk) | Lowest temperature while running drifts toward 0 °C; long runs at low ambient | Yes, hours to days |
| Sensor fault | Physically implausible jumps; disagreement with the twin and a second probe | Detectable |

Gradient-boosted trees on these engineered features make a strong, explainable baseline, because SHAP contributions map onto physical causes.

### No study reports fridge excursion lead times, so simple baselines come first

No peer-reviewed work was found that forecasts refrigerator excursions with explicit lead times, alert precision or false-alarm rates. The ESP32 vaccine-fridge autoencoder detects faults rather than forecasting them ([Harrabi 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11324578/)). An agricultural cold-chain LSTM–XGBoost ensemble forecasts temperature but gives no alarm metrics ([PeerJ CS 2024](https://peerj.com/articles/cs-2510.pdf)). Building-temperature studies are the nearest analogue. On the Smart Buildings Control Suite, classical baselines captured short-term dynamics well, and Transformers led only on multi-week horizons ([NeurIPS 2025 workshop](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/136646)). DLinear showed a single linear layer beating many Transformer variants on long-horizon benchmarks ([Zeng et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)). At ThermoTwin's 15–60-minute horizon, short-term dynamics dominate. That favours persistence, the RC twin and LightGBM over deep sequence models. A patent application also notes that plain LSTMs lag during door-opening disturbances ([CN122508969A](https://eureka.patsnap.com/patent/CN122508969A)).

Two framings fit the task. One is direct classification: will product temperature cross the WHO threshold within h minutes? The other is forecast-then-threshold, using a quantile forecaster's probability of exceeding the threshold. A discrete-time hazard model unifies both and also yields a time-to-breach estimate. Dynamic survival analysis with a thresholded alarm policy is the closest published template ([Yèche et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12818)).

### Unsupervised anomaly detectors mistake doors and defrosts for faults

On a 30-day pharmaceutical cold-chain dataset of about 8,640 five-minute readings, Isolation Forest beat autoencoder and LSTM models. Even so, it reached only **precision 0.686, recall 0.418 and F1 0.52** ([JTMI 2026](https://jurnal.unmer.ac.id/index.php/jtmi/article/view/17062)). A modified Isolation Forest on logistics streams reached F1 0.86 ([PMC11892858](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11892858/)). Door openings and defrost cycles produce temperature rises between normal compressor cycles that look anomalous. That is why commercial monitors simply lengthen alarm delays, for example from 30 to 60–90 minutes ([US10989464](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10989464)). The wider literature warns that popular anomaly benchmarks are flawed by trivial or mislabelled anomalies ([Wu & Keogh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13807)). It also warns that the point-adjust protocol inflates F1 so much that random scores can look state-of-the-art ([Kim et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05257)). Unsupervised detection should therefore be only a secondary novelty signal on RC-twin residuals, with known door and defrost periods masked, never the primary alarm.

### Residual learning on an RC model holds up out of distribution

In a 2025 EPFL comparison of five hybrid approaches to probabilistic indoor-temperature modelling, residual learning performed best on average. This approach puts a feed-forward network on top of a physics model. It was also the only approach that produced physically sensible predictions on out-of-distribution data, and quantile conformal prediction calibrated its intervals ([EPFL, arXiv:2507.17526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17526v1)). A 4R3C model plus MLP roughly halved error, from 0.851 °C MAE for physics alone to 0.454 °C, though only within the training distribution ([CESI LINEACT](https://lineact.cesi.fr/en/?p=194063)). Physically consistent neural networks build physical behaviour into the architecture ([Di Natale et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2112.03212)). No refrigeration-specific out-of-distribution comparison exists, but the building evidence is consistent: physics carries the extrapolation and ML corrects the residual. The hackathon's training and hidden test data are likely simulated. If so, an RC twin of the simulator's model family plus a residual learner is the most robust bet against shifts in ambient, loading or fault severity. A differentiable RC model fitted by gradient descent captures most of the benefit of a full physics-informed network.

### Foundation models are a zero-shot baseline, not the engine

Among time-series foundation models, Chronos-Bolt-Base scores an aggregate relative MASE of 0.791 on a 25-dataset zero-shot benchmark. That puts it ahead of Moirai-Base (0.812) and TimesFM (0.879) ([arXiv:2605.07222](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07222)). It also has consistently low energy use and latency ([Guibert et al. 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v309/guibert26a.html)). Rankings shift across domains on live benchmarks ([LiveHouse-TS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17299)). TimesFM v1 carries a self-declared test-leakage flag ([arXiv:2605.07222](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07222)), and TimeGPT is API-only ([Garza & Mergenthaler-Canseco](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03589)). No study has applied these models to refrigerator temperature. A thermostatic sawtooth driven by door and compressor events is a poor fit for univariate zero-shot forecasting. None of these models runs on an ESP32-class microcontroller, where a 1.2 MB autoencoder is the realistic ceiling ([Harrabi 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11324578/)). Chronos-Bolt is worth showing as a baseline on a gateway, not as the core.

### Calibrated probabilities and event-level scoring make claims credible

Boosted trees produce distorted probabilities. Platt scaling suits small calibration sets and isotonic regression suits larger ones ([Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana 2005](https://doi.org/10.1145/1102351.1102430)). For forecast intervals under drift, adaptive conformal inference keeps long-run coverage by adjusting one parameter online ([Gibbs & Candès 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00170v3)). A parameter-free variant (AgACI) and an ensemble method (EnbPI) are alternatives ([Zaffran et al. 2022](https://icml.cc/virtual/2022/spotlight/17818); [Xu & Xie](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.09107)). Alerts must be scored as events, not points. The Numenta Anomaly Benchmark credits only the earliest detection in a window and penalises false positives outside it ([Lavin & Ahmad 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.03336)). Range-based precision and recall add a front-end bias that rewards early detection ([Tatbul et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03639)). Consecutive alert points must be merged into episodes before false alarms are counted. The alert threshold should be chosen to meet an explicit false-alert budget on validation data.

### MKT and VVM budgets translate temperature into product risk

The VVM categories embed an Arrhenius relationship. For VVM30, VVM14 and VVM7, the ratio of days-to-discard at +25 °C to days at +37 °C is the same, about 6.43 (193/30, 90/14, 45/7) ([DCVMN](https://dcvmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/choice-of-vvm-category.pdf)). A calculation made for this report from those published end points implies a common apparent activation energy of about **119 kJ/mol**, well above USP's 83.144 kJ/mol default. A ThermoTwin "stability budget" can track consumed life by summing each time interval divided by the end-point time at that temperature, scaled from the +37 °C end point. Using 119 kJ/mol reproduces the VVM +25 °C end points. Anchored at +37 °C, the USP default is conservative below that temperature and optimistic above it, so both values should be shown with the assumption stated. A rolling 24-hour MKT, the USP cold-chain criterion, shown beside a VVM-equivalent life counter gives health workers a product-relevant number. Freeze exposure needs its own counter, because no Arrhenius budget captures freezing damage.

### Open data is scarce, so test against a second simulator

No large, open, labelled refrigerator-excursion dataset exists, and no open vaccine-refrigerator log dataset was found. Usable substitutes include ORNL's display-case faults ([Sun et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8159987/)) and the Benamirouche synthetic fridges with their generator ([arXiv:2609.22229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22229)). REFIT offers two years of 8-second appliance power data from 20 UK homes, including fridges and freezers, which is useful for realistic compressor cycling ([Murray et al. 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5315495/)). A Bangkok dataset records in-fridge temperature and humidity at 5-minute intervals from 123 households ([B2FIND](https://b2find.eudat.eu/dataset/43ac660a-fa48-5675-a56c-fb3252b86fa0)). Licences must be confirmed on each landing page. An Edge Impulse developer reported finding no open in-fridge dataset and building one ([Edge Impulse](https://docs.edgeimpulse.com/experts/refrigerator-predictive-maintenance)). The most useful robustness test is to train on the team's own simulator and test on the Benamirouche generator. That approximates the risk of a hidden test set built by someone else.

## Alarm-fatigue evidence favours fewer, earlier, tiered alerts

Process-industry alarm standards give the clearest numeric targets. The texts are paywalled, so the figures here come from published summaries. ISA-18.2 treats about 150 alarms per operator per day (about 6 per hour) as very likely acceptable, and about 300 as the manageable maximum ([Yokogawa](https://www.yokogawa.com/ch/library/resources/media-publications/implementing-alarm-management-per-the-ansi-isa-182-standard-control-engineering/)). Tables derived from EEMUA set further targets. Floods (more than 10 alarms in 10 minutes) should occur in fewer than 1% of 10-minute periods. There should be no chattering alarms and fewer than 5 stale alarms, meaning alarms active for over 24 hours. The ten most frequent alarms should make up only about 1–5% of the total ([Emerson](https://d1-live.emerson.com/documents/automation/article-alarm-management-by-numbers-deltav-en-38292.pdf); [IChemE 2021](https://www.icheme.org/media/16019/icheme-alarm-management-practical-guide-06_05_2021.pdf)). Annunciated priorities should split roughly 80% low, 15% medium and 5% high ([exida Solvay](https://www.exida.com/Case-Study/solvay-plant-reduces-downtime-and-improves-safety-through-alarm-management)), because when too many alarms are high priority, none effectively is ([exida](https://exida.com/Blog/which-measure-rationalized-or-annunciated-is-more-important)). Temperature alarms get a deadband of about 1% of span ([exida deadband](https://www.exida.com/Blog/why-should-i-use-an-alarm-deadband)). Shelving must be time-limited, operator-initiated and listed, while "suppression by design" follows the state of the process ([ProcessOnline](https://www.processonline.com.au/content/software-it/article/improving-alarm-management-with-isa-18-2-part-2-1141142642)).

A clinic nurse or pharmacist is a part-time operator with other duties. For a fridge fleet the right budget is therefore a handful of actionable alerts per week, not per hour. On a 2–8 °C fridge, a 1% deadband (about 0.1–0.2 °C) is smaller than the ±0.5 °C sensor uncertainty that CDC accepts. Hysteresis should be at least the sensor uncertainty.

Clinical monitoring supplies the fatigue evidence, by analogy. The Joint Commission's Sentinel Event Alert 50 estimated that **85–99% of alarm signals need no clinical intervention**, and blamed limits set too tight and defaults left unadjusted. It recorded 98 alarm-related sentinel events from 2009 to mid-2012, 80 of them deaths. Alarm fatigue was the most common contributing factor, and in 36 events alarms had been inappropriately turned off ([TJC SEA 50](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/f65e5c9df2b94000a99445e0a7877007)). In five UCSF ICUs, Drew et al. logged **2.56 million alarms in 31 days**, or 187 audible alarms per bed per day. They found 88.8% of 12,671 annotated arrhythmia alarms were false positives ([Drew et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4206416)). The often-quoted 72–99% false-alarm range comes from a review of bedside monitoring ([review citing Sendelbach & Funk](https://www.academia.edu/98739208/Nurses_Attitudes_Related_To_Alarm_Fatigue_in_Critical_Care_Units_A_Systematic_Review)). None of this measures cold-chain alarms. A Rutgers dissertation notes that research on alarm fatigue from non-patient alarms is missing ([Rutgers](https://rucore.libraries.rutgers.edu/rutgers-lib/65271/)), and no peer-reviewed study of fatigue from vaccine-fridge alarms was found.

The same literature quantifies what reduces false alarms. A 15-second SpO2 delay cut false alarms by an estimated 60%, and in a trauma unit, 2- and 5-second delays cut alarms by 25% and 49%. Adaptive delays cut false positives by 73.5% in a retrospective study, and revised default limits cut critical alarms by 43% ([Arora et al. review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.03885); [UKE Hamburg](https://fis.uke.de/portal/en/publications/reduction-of-clinically-irrelevant-alarms-in-patient-monitoring-by-adaptive-time-delays(99275c89-3a53-490d-b14d-65976f65969b).html)). Multi-signal fusion does best. A random-forest classifier suppressed **90.3% of false tachycardia alarms while wrongly suppressing only 0.54% of true ones** ([Arora et al. review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.03885)). A Johns Hopkins randomised study of altered settings found fewer alarms and no difference in adverse events ([FierceHealthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/healthcare/three-lessons-learned-to-reduce-alarm-fatigue-and-improve-alarm-management-and-patient)). Every credible reduction claim comes with a safety metric: the true-alarm suppression rate, held below about 1%.

Cold-chain evidence is thinner but points the same way. CDC and WHO already build the two main defences against false alarms into measurement: thermal buffering and persistence. In a pharmacy fridge whose compressor cycled hourly, unbuffered air swung between 1.7 and 6.2 °C over 24 hours, while a 20-ml glycol bottle stayed between 3.6 and 4.3 °C. An algorithmic "virtual buffer" tracked the glycol to within a mean of −0.078 °C, and needed at least one sample every 10.8 minutes ([Rusnack 2018](https://pubs.lib.umn.edu/index.php/innovations/article/download/962/1050/2207); the author works for a vendor).

Nexleaf's randomised evaluation in Mozambique ran in 2014–15. It compared 29 facilities with remote monitoring and SMS alerts, 28 with 30-DTRs and 26 with stem thermometers. Its alerts fired deliberately earlier than WHO's: below +2 °C for 30 minutes and above +8 °C for 60 minutes. The remote-monitoring group achieved **90% uptime, against 86% and 74%**, and spent 2 hours a month in the WHO freeze-alarm state, against 4 and 6 ([Nexleaf RCT report](https://www.nexleaf.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Nexleaf-RTM-RCT-Report.pdf)). Heat did not improve: the remote-monitoring group spent more time in warm alert and heat alarm than the 30-DTR group (45 and 18 hours, against 36 and 15). Nexleaf concluded that many chronically broken fridges kept failing despite alerts, and shifted its focus to remote diagnosis and maintenance ([Nexleaf IoT series 2017](https://www.nexleaf.org/wp-content/uploads/reports/IoT-Series_ColdTrace-PDF-Report_2017.pdf)).

A 2011 SMS alarm pilot in Albania produced more than 130 alarms at 24 sites in 10 months. Supervisors phoned staff in 41% of incidents, and evaluators found no case where remote alarms saved vaccine, so the cost-benefit case was not made ([PATH/WHO Optimize](https://media.path.org/documents/TS_opt_temp_monitor.pdf)). In Belize, 8 devices produced 18 alarms in 8 months, about **0.28 alarms per device per month**. Alarms lasted 0.6–31.3 hours (mean 9.6), and 75% were resolved within 24 hours ([Oladeji et al. 2025](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/view/13600)). In other words, slow response, not alarm volume, was the bottleneck.

Human-factors guidance converges on alerts that are actionable, owned by a named person, and escalated. CDC's excursion record works as a message template. It covers the unit, temperatures including min/max, duration, affected lots, contributing factors such as a cracked seal, and how long it took to act ([CDC Toolkit 2026](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/downloads/storage-handling-toolkit.pdf)). The Joint Commission's alarm-safety goal requires policies on settings, on who may change them, and on when alarms may be disabled ([Relias Media](https://reliasmedia.com/articles/64545-new-npsg-addresses-clinical-alarm-safety)). Nexleaf credited its freezing reduction to SMS escalated to supervisors, combined with training and posted SOPs ([Nexleaf RCT report](https://www.nexleaf.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Nexleaf-RTM-RCT-Report.pdf)). Vendors warn that alarm delays must not mask a stuck defrost, which causes genuinely high temperatures ([Envigilance](https://envigilance.com/temperature-monitoring/walk-in-freezer-monitoring/)).

The implied ThermoTwin alert policy rests on four choices. First, **alarm on product temperature** from a virtual buffer, not on raw air temperature. Second, **copy WHO's deliberate asymmetry**, which is fast for freezing and slow for heat. That means an early-warning tier at Nexleaf's levels and a critical tier at WHO-grade persistence. Third, **treat forecasts as low-priority advisories** that add lead time rather than volume. A forecast should be promoted to SMS only when it is a high-confidence, short-horizon freeze forecast, or when a rule tier trips. Fourth, **suppress door and defrost transients only by logged design**, and re-arm when a transient lasts longer than the unit's learned recovery time. Belize's alarm rate is a sobering yardstick. An SMS-tier false-alert budget of one per fridge per week would mean roughly fifteen times the total alarm volume Belize staff handled. A budget closer to one false alert per fridge per month is the defensible ceiling. This is a judgement derived from that figure, not a published standard.

## LLM agents earn a place only as explainers and drafters

Industrial vendors now ship LLM copilots for maintenance. What actually ships is bounded language tasks, and the evidence for it is vendor-reported. Siemens added generative-AI repair guidance on top of Senseye in March 2025 ([Automation.com](https://www.automation.com/en-us/products/march-2025/siemens-industrial-copilot-generative-ai)). IBM's Maximo Condition Insight gives plain-language recommendations, while Maximo's production AI features suggest failure codes, detect duplicate work orders and validate entries ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/new/announcements/maximo-condition-insight); [IBM Expert Labs](https://www.ibm.com/new/announcements/introducing-ai-offerings-for-maximo-application-suite-from-ibm-expert-labs)). AWS's IoT SiteWise Assistant, generally available since November 2024, summarises alarms and retrieves SOPs from the customer's own data ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2024/11/aws-iot-sitewise-generative-ai-powered-industrial-assistant)). Microsoft's Factory Operations Agent, still in preview, answers root-cause questions and checks its results against source data ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/pt-br/industry/manufacturing/manufacturing-data-solutions/overview-copilot-factory-operations)). An AWS reference design for utility alarm floods claims it cuts triage from 40–60 minutes of manual work to a few minutes, but publishes no accuracy data ([AWS for Industries](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/intelligent-alarm-management-for-power-and-utilities-using-amazon-bedrock-agentcore/)). No vaccine or pharma cold-chain monitoring vendor offering a generative-AI copilot was found.

Independent benchmarks show why detection and decisions must stay out of the LLM. IBM's AssetOpsBench spans 141 maintenance scenarios and 2.3 million sensor points. It uses four specialist agents, covering telemetry, failure modes, forecasting and work orders. In the plan-and-execute pattern, the best model completed only **46.5% of tasks**, and two open models hallucinated more than 63% of the time. Removing worked examples from the prompt dropped GPT-4.1 from 80% to 34% on single-agent tasks. The failure analysis counted 122 cases (23.8%) of agents claiming a task had succeeded when it had not ([AssetOpsBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03828v1)). Even the benchmark's LLM judge agreed with human experts only 69–75% of the time. A widely shared claim that accuracy fell from 68% to 47% with multiple agents could not be verified against the paper. On real-world root-cause analysis, the best model solved 11.34% of OpenRCA cases ([OpenRCA, ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/d29b8d53678015079e1d245c023e49d2-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Across 11 frontier models in OpenRCA 2.0, agents found the exact set of root causes in 20.7% of cases on average, and grounded their answer in a verified causal path in 61.5% ([OpenRCA 2.0 summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2606.27154)).

LLMs are also weak at the core numeric task. Removing the language model from three popular LLM forecasters did not hurt their accuracy ([Tan et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16964v1)). Strong LLMs reasoned about time series only slightly better than chance ([Merrill et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.11757v1)). LLM-based anomaly detection trailed specialised detectors by about 30% ([SigLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14755v1)). What works is LLMs operating over features extracted by tools, plus domain context. One example is an agent that writes detection rules which then run deterministically ([Argos](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.14170)). Another is root-cause agents that benefit substantially from domain context ([TraceBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27182)).

For ThermoTwin, this argues for one orchestrator calling five to eight typed tools rather than a team of autonomous agents. AssetOpsBench found that interleaving tool calls was more reliable than planning first and then delegating ([AssetOpsBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03828v1)). The monitoring "agent" should not be an LLM at all but a deterministic loop on the edge device. The explanation role receives a compact evidence packet. It contains door-open minutes, compressor current, mains state, ambient temperature, temperature slope, and predicted time-to-breach with its probability. It returns schema-validated JSON that picks a cause from a fixed list and cites the feature values behind it. Escalation stays rule-based: the LLM may word a message but never decides whether to send it. Every claimed action, such as "technician notified", must be checked against the tool's return value. Code computes every number that appears in the text, and curated worked examples stay in the prompt. Drafting maintenance tickets and writing weekly fridge-health reports in Hindi or a regional language are good LLM fits.

Consequential actions need human confirmation. These include discarding stock, marking it unusable and escalating to district officers. ThermoTwin should never itself recommend using or discarding vaccine; national guidelines decide that from VVM status, shake tests and the excursion record. WHO's 2024 guidance on large multimodal models in health calls for oversight, independent post-release auditing and early involvement of health workers ([WHO LMM guidance](https://www.who.int/news/item/18-01-2024-who-releases-ai-ethics-and-governance-guidance-for-large-multi-modal-models)). Logging each explanation alongside its evidence and the human action taken provides that audit trail.

## Offline-first ESP32 nodes and SMS-first alerts fit Indian facilities

Compute is not the constraint at the edge. A four-tree, depth-four LightGBM model ran in **17.63 µs per prediction** on an ESP32-S3 ([Boosted Trees on a Diet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26557)). emlearn converts scikit-learn trees into dependency-free C99 code, tested on ESP32 and Cortex-M ([emlearn](https://emlearn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tree_based_models.html)). TensorFlow Lite Micro's core runtime fits in 16 KB on a Cortex-M3 ([TensorFlow](https://www.tensorflow.org/lite/microcontrollers?hl=pt)), and tree ensembles use far less energy and memory than deep networks ([PACSET](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.05383)).

Power, connectivity and maintenance are the real constraints. eVIN's design is a field-proven template: 10-minute readings, hourly GPRS upload, a local audible alarm, SMS on breach, 24-hour battery backup and dual-SIM loggers for patchy networks ([Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/health/health-care-innovations/health-system-strengthening-1/evin-project-of-health-ministry-health-system-strengthening-1-1?lgn=en); [Ahmedabad Smart City](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/e-vin_health_by_ahmedabad_smartcity_.pdf)). Support is thin. One report cites a deep-freezer maintenance budget of ₹1,500 per district per year ([Governance Now](https://www.governancenow.com/gov-next/egov/the-shot-just-got-smarter-evin-vaccine-immunisation)).

Connectivity options are uneven. The 865–867 MHz band is licence-free for LoRaWAN in India, and a private gateway reportedly costs ₹10,000–20,000 and can cover a village. Generic 868 MHz modules, however, may default to channels outside India's band ([ThinkRobotics](https://thinkrobotics.com/blogs/learn/lorawan-gateway-setup-india-complete-deployment-guide-for-2025); [Zbotic](https://zbotic.in/nb-iot-vs-lte-m-vs-lora-low-power-wan-comparison-for-india-2025/)). Vendor blogs report that NB-IoT is concentrated in metros and that LTE-M is not widely available, though no operator confirmation was found ([Zbotic](https://zbotic.in/nb-iot-vs-lte-m-vs-lora-low-power-wan-comparison-for-india-2025/)). Store-and-forward must be durable. AWS Greengrass v2's Stream Manager persists data to disk ([AWS Greengrass v2](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/greengrass/v2/developerguide/manage-data-streams.html)), whereas the default MQTT spooler is a 2.5 MB in-memory cache that drops the oldest messages ([AWS re:Post](https://repost.aws/it/questions/QUaMSDu7d6T6iHFKa5zOZcAA/guaranteed-delivery-of-events-with-intermittently-offline-device)). Greengrass v1 support ended on 7 October 2026 ([AWS Greengrass v1](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/greengrass/v1/developerguide/stream-manager.md)).

Sensor choice drives both credibility and false alarms. WHO's 30-DTR specification demands ±0.5 °C from −20 to +20 °C and traceable calibration ([WHO PQS E006/TR06.3](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/PQS_E006_TR06.3.pdf)). The common DS18B20 meets ±0.5 °C only between −10 and +85 °C, with no margin ([Analog Devices](https://support.analog.com/en-US//knowledgebase/article/000095991)). The SHT31 offers about ±0.2 °C typical from 0 to 90 °C ([DFRobot](https://wiki.dfrobot.com/SHT31%20Digital%20Temperature%20and%20Humidity%20Sensor%20SKU%3A%20SEN0331)). Neither covers −15 to −25 °C freezers within specification; those need an RTD. A split-core SCT-013 current clamp (±1%) on the fridge supply reveals compressor duty cycle and power loss without touching the wiring. A 5–20 A variant suits small compressors ([Zbotic SCT-013](https://zbotic.in/product/sct-013-060-non-invasive-ac-current-sensor-clamp-sensor-60a/)). Adding a door reed switch and mains detection makes most cause attribution nearly rule-based. A prototype built from these parts is not prequalified. It should be pitched as a predictive add-on beside the certified PQS or eVIN logger, with its probe in a buffer vial.

Alert channels in India should be layered. eVIN already relies on a local alarm plus SMS and email ([Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/health/health-care-innovations/health-system-strengthening-1/evin-project-of-health-ministry-health-system-strengthening-1-1?lgn=en)). India's national Kilkari voice programme shows that retries matter. **50% of calls were answered on the first attempt, 76% by the third and 99.5% by the ninth** ([Kilkari analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.07139)). Learning the best call time significantly improved pickup in a field trial of about 6,500 participants ([Dasgupta et al. 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v298/dasgupta25a.html)). ASHAs use WhatsApp heavily for work, though qualitative research questions the extra workload ([CHW Central](https://chwcentral.org/wp-content/uploads/More-work-for-ASHAs-Exploring-the-role-of-whatsapp-messaging-and-community-health-work-in-urban-slum-areas-of-Varanasi-India.pdf)). Microsoft Research's ASHABot shows LLM assistants reaching frontline workers through WhatsApp in Hindi ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1141120)).

Two rules shape message design. First, TRAI's DLT regime blocks commercial SMS that does not match a pre-registered template, so free-form LLM text cannot go out by SMS ([Telerivet](https://www.telerivet.com/blog/india-sms-compliance-trai-dlt-registration-and-tcccpr-guide)). Second, WhatsApp moved to per-message pricing on 1 July 2025. Utility templates are reported at about ₹0.11–0.13 each, and rate cards were updated in April 2026 ([Exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/marketing-news/whatsapp-introduces-permessage-billing-for-business-messaging-144935.html); [Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)). No head-to-head study of SMS, WhatsApp and voice response for cold-chain alerts exists. The defensible escalation ladder starts with a local buzzer that works with no network. Next comes a DLT-templated SMS carrying fridge ID, temperature, predicted time-to-breach and a cause code. If that goes unacknowledged, a Hindi or regional-language voice call follows, with three to five retries. The alert then escalates to the medical officer and the district cold-chain officer. WhatsApp carries the LLM explanation, a chart and SOP steps, as enrichment rather than as the alarm itself.

Privacy is manageable. Temperature telemetry is not personal data, but recipients' names and phone numbers are. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified on 13–14 November 2025, and most consent and notice obligations take effect around May 2027 ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). Storing only role, phone and facility, and sending only telemetry features to any cloud LLM, keeps ThermoTwin's personal-data footprint minimal. Whether CDSCO would treat the software as a medical device was not researched.

## Conclusion and design implications for ThermoTwin

The research moves ThermoTwin's value away from "monitoring", which India already does at scale with roughly 25,000 SIM loggers. It points instead to two things the field demonstrably lacks. The first is lead time inside the window that WHO's persistence rules leave unalarmed. The second is diagnosis that turns repeated alarms into repairs. Nexleaf's own trial showed that alerts reduced freezing but not chronic equipment failure, and Albania and Belize showed that more alarms do not save vaccine when response is slow. The honest benchmark is therefore not the 10-hour WHO heat alarm, which is trivial to beat. It is the earlier rule-based alerts that Nexleaf and eVIN already use: below +2 °C and above +8 °C. ThermoTwin must show lead time over those, freezing first. It must do so at a false-alert rate low enough not to swamp a facility that, in Belize, saw about one alarm per fridge every three to four months.

The most persuasive asset for the pitch is a replay benchmark with published metrics, because no competitor publishes any. Run three policies over the same traces: a naive instantaneous air-temperature threshold, a Nexleaf-style early warning, and ThermoTwin's policy. Then report recall on WHO-grade events, true-alarm suppression, median lead time and false alerts per fridge-month. Combined with WHO's 2026 EMS mandate and the DS01.2 JSON schema, this positions ThermoTwin as a vendor-neutral analytics layer. OEMs, RTMD providers or state immunisation programmes could adopt it before Copeland, Sensitech or Digi add short-horizon prediction to the stationary monitors they have just acquired.
