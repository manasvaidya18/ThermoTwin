ThermoTwin

A digital twin for every vaccine fridge. It predicts cold-chain excursions before the standard alarms fire, freezing first. It explains the cause in the health worker's own language, and it turns repeated alarms into verified repairs.

Research, part 1The problem, as the evidence shows it

The research changed the framing. The measured risk in India is mostly silent freezing, not heat. The standard alarms are designed to fire late. And India already has connected loggers whose data is used for stock, not for prediction.

What this means for ThermoTwin

Freezing is the silent killer

Vaccine vial monitors record only heat, so freeze damage leaves no visible mark. Only about 40% of service-level health workers understand the shake test. Freeze-sensitive vaccines include pentavalent, hepatitis B, HPV, PCV, TT/Td and IPV. ThermoTwin predicts both directions and treats freezing as the urgent one.

The standards leave a window open

Under default rules a fridge can sit above +8 °C for almost 10 hours, or below −0.5 °C for almost an hour, before any alarm. Forecasting the time to a WHO-grade alarm is the job the standards leave undone. WHO's own catalogue names "predictive failure responses" as the goal.

The data already exists

India's eVIN runs about 25,000 SIM loggers at about 29,000 cold-chain points, reading every 10 minutes and uploading hourly. Its published outcomes concern stock-outs, not excursions. ThermoTwin is an analytics layer on that telemetry, with a cheap add-on node where no logger exists.

Alerts alone do not fix fridges

In a Mozambique trial, SMS monitoring cut time in the WHO freeze-alarm state from 4–6 to 2 hours a month, but chronically broken fridges kept failing. Slow response, not alarm volume, is the bottleneck. So ThermoTwin diagnoses the cause and closes the repair loop.

Why now

From 2026 every vaccine refrigerator must carry a WHO-standard equipment monitoring system, and the WHO data standard (DS01.2) defines alarm codes and a JSON format for raw data. That gives ThermoTwin ready-made prediction targets and a vendor-neutral input format. Close to a billion people are served by health facilities with no or unreliable electricity, so power cuts are a constant, not an exception.

Closest analogues (say this honestly)

Emanate PowerPath Temp (2016–17) used machine learning on power draw to learn defrost and door patterns and issue predictive alarms for hospital fridges; its current status is unknown. Predictive-maintenance vendors do condition monitoring for supermarkets. An ESP32 autoencoder paper (Harrabi 2024) detects vaccine-fridge faults but does not forecast. So we claim the combination, not the idea of prediction.

Our white space

Short-horizon excursion forecasting, freezing first, with automatic cause attribution and a measured false-alarm budget. It runs on low-cost, low-connectivity hardware and sits as a vendor-neutral layer beside the certified logger. It reads WHO DS01.2 JSON or 10-minute eVIN-style logs. We will publish our lead time and false-alert numbers, which no competitor does.

The window is closing

Sensitech bought Berlinger's monitoring business (2024). Copeland now owns Dickson (completed September 2026) and agreed to buy Bueno Analytics. Digi bought Jolt and launched SmartSense ONE with "predictive insights". Nexleaf has the largest LMIC fridge dataset and door sensors in the field. Any of them could add short-horizon prediction, so ThermoTwin's position is "open, vendor-neutral, measured, and built for India's existing telemetry".

Our answerEight ideas that make ThermoTwin different

Freeze-first

Two-sided prediction, freezing treated as urgent

Most teams will predict "temperature rising". India's measured damage is mostly freezing, which nobody can see afterwards. ThermoTwin forecasts both edges of the 2 to 8 °C band and fast-tracks freeze risk, mirroring WHO's own asymmetry (60 minutes for freeze, 10 hours for heat).

Physics plus ML

A digital twin of every fridge, ML on what physics misses

Each unit gets a small grey-box thermal model whose parameters are learned online. It forecasts physically, and its drifting parameters reveal causes: a leaking gasket, weak cooling, a drifting thermostat. Gradient-boosted trees learn the residual and the risk.

Three clocks

Breach clock, WHO clock, holdover clock

Minutes until the product leaves 2 to 8 °C. Hours until a WHO-grade alarm would fire. During a power cut, hours of holdover left. Staff act on countdowns, not on raw temperatures.

Two-key alerts

ML and physics must agree before anyone's phone rings

A forecast becomes an SMS only when the ML hazard and the twin's own rollout both predict a breach. Clinical studies show multi-signal fusion removed up to 90% of false alarms. WHO-grade rule alarms are never suppressed.

Product, not air

Virtual Vial and the Vaccine Life Meter

Alarms run on a simulated product temperature, like the glycol-buffered probe CDC requires, so door-opening air spikes do not page anyone. A life meter shows rolling 24-hour MKT, vial-monitor-equivalent heat used, and a separate freeze-exposure counter.

Fix loop

From alarm to verified repair

A Cause Card explains what is wrong. A ticket with the evidence goes to the technician. After the repair, the twin checks that cooling strength or seal health is back to baseline and closes the loop: "Repair verified".

Bounded agent

An AI copilot that explains and drafts, but never decides

One orchestrator calls typed tools to write the Cause Card in Hindi or Marathi, guide staff through the SOP, draft maintenance tickets and weekly district digests. Detection and escalation stay deterministic, because agent benchmarks show low root-cause accuracy.

Published scorecard

A replay benchmark nobody else publishes

We run four alerting policies over the same traces (naive threshold, WHO default, Nexleaf-style early alerts, ThermoTwin) and publish lead time, recall on WHO-grade events, true-alarm suppression and false alerts per fridge-month.

The two-key rule

forecast_alert(unit, t) =
      trust(t)            >= 0.7                                   # data is believable
  AND P_breach(<= 30 min) >= theta_budget(unit)                    # ML key, threshold set by the false-alert budget
  AND twin_rollout_as_is crosses the band within 45 min             # physics key
  AND NOT valid_suppression(unit, t)                                # door, restock or defrost window still open

freeze_fast_track(unit, t) =
      trust(t) >= 0.7 AND P(product < 0 C within 30 min) >= theta_freeze   # lower threshold, freeze-first

rule_alerts (never suppressed):  WHO FRZE  product or logger <= -0.5 C for 60 min
                                 WHO HEAT  >= +8 C for 10 h
early rule tiers:                < +2 C for 30 min, > +8 C for 60 min, door open > 5 min, power off with holdover < 4 h

Suppression with re-arm (so delays never hide a real fault)

  • Known contexts open a suppression window: door session, restocking, a scheduled defrost. Only Advisory and Early tiers are suppressed, never Critical.
  • The window length is the unit's own learned recovery time τrec from the twin, times 1.5. If the fridge has not recovered by then, the alert re-arms immediately. This stops a stuck defrost or a door left ajar from hiding behind a delay.
  • Every suppression is logged with its reason. Manual shelving expires automatically after 2 hours and is always listed on the dashboard.

Alarm budget controller

  • On validation traces, θbudget is the lowest threshold that keeps SMS-tier false alerts at or below 1 per fridge per month. That ceiling is our judgement, based on the Belize deployment's real rate of about 0.28 alarms per device per month.
  • The safety counterweight is the true-alarm suppression rate: alerts we hold back that turn out to be real must stay below 1%, the safety margin used by clinical alarm-reduction studies.
  • In deployment, adaptive conformal inference nudges each unit's threshold so its realised false-alert rate tracks the budget.

Feedback loop and cold start

Learning from staff

Every acknowledgement can carry a one-tap tag: restocking, door was open, power cut, technician visit, false alarm. Tags become labels; each week the unit's calibration and suppression windows update. Disagreements between tags and the model are queued for review.

A new fridge on day one

Start from priors for its equipment class (WHO E003 holdover and capacity figures), run the global model and the rule tiers, and identify the twin over the first 48 to 72 hours of normal cycling. Forecast alerts switch on once the twin's 20-minute error is stable.

Deliberately not the core
  • A deep LSTM as the main model. No evidence of better lead time at this horizon, harder to explain, and a patent notes plain LSTMs lag during door disturbances. Kept as a baseline.
  • Unsupervised anomaly detection as the alarm. On a pharma cold-chain dataset the best detector reached F1 0.52; door openings and defrost cycles look anomalous. Used only as a secondary signal on twin residuals with doors and defrost masked.
  • An LLM for detection. LLMs reason about time series only slightly better than chance, and root-cause agents solve 11 to 21% of benchmark cases.
  • Time-series foundation models as the engine. None has been tested on fridge data or runs on a microcontroller. Chronos-Bolt is shown as a zero-shot baseline only.

Cause list (fixed, so explanations stay honest)

Door left open · power loss · compressor or refrigerant · gasket leak · evaporator icing or missed defrost · thermostat drift (freeze) · high ambient · overload or warm restock · condenser fouling · sensor fault.

Repair verified

When a technician marks a repair done, the twin compares the relevant Vital over the next 24 hours with the unit's baseline band (90 to 110%). In band: "Repair verified" and the ticket closes. Out of band: the ticket reopens with the evidence. This turns Nexleaf's lesson (broken fridges keep failing) into a measurable loop.

Why one orchestrator, not a swarm

AssetOpsBench found interleaving tool calls was more reliable than planning first and delegating to many agents. So ThermoTwin has one orchestrator with typed tools. The "agents" above are jobs, not autonomous actors.

Models and languages

Any capable API model with structured output (declare which), or a small open model on the gateway for offline use. Indian-language translation and speech through Bhashini or a cloud text-to-speech service. Only telemetry features leave the facility; no personal data goes to the LLM.

How we evaluate the copilot: 100% of numbers match tool outputs on 100 test alerts; 100% of causes from the allowed list; native speakers rate the Hindi and Marathi Cause Cards; staff rate usefulness on a 1 to 5 scale.

Who is affected

Slide 3 · Proposed Solution and Innovation

What makes it different

  • Freeze-first, two-sided forecasting with three clocks: breach, WHO alarm, power-cut holdover.
  • A physics digital twin of every fridge, with ML learning what the physics misses.
  • Two-key alerts: ML and physics must agree before a phone rings, within a false-alert budget of 1 per fridge per month.

Viva preparationQuestions judges will ask

Why not just lower the alarm threshold?

That is what vendors' "warnings" do, and it adds noise. Lower thresholds still fire after the trend starts and fire on every door opening. We forecast, use product temperature instead of air, and require ML and physics to agree, under a stated false-alert budget.

Why focus on freezing?

Indian logger studies measured vaccine below 0 °C for 10.5 to 14.8% of storage time, and vial monitors cannot show freezing. WHO's own rules already treat freezing as more urgent (60 minutes versus 10 hours).

Your data is simulated. Why should we trust it?

We randomise equipment, seasons, power and faults, test on unseen fault combinations and on a second, independent generator, and state the simulator's limits. The twin learns each real fridge's physics online, so field use starts with a short calibration period.

What is new here? Monitoring already exists.

Monitoring exists; forecasting with cause and a measured false-alarm rate does not, in any product we found for stationary vaccine storage. The closest analogue (Emanate, 2017) targeted hospital fridges and its status is unknown. We publish our numbers; no vendor does.

Where is the AI, and why not a big deep-learning model?

The AI is the hybrid twin plus calibrated gradient boosting, chosen because the evidence favours it at a 15 to 60 minute horizon and because it runs on a microcontroller and explains itself. A TCN and a time-series foundation model are included as baselines.

Can an LLM hallucinate a wrong diagnosis to a health worker?

The LLM picks a cause from a fixed list, every number comes from a tool, a verifier checks each output, and if any check fails we send a template. It never decides whether to alert, and never advises using or discarding vaccine.

How do you avoid false alarms from door openings and defrost?

Product temperature instead of air, door and current signals, logged suppression windows with automatic re-arm after the unit's own recovery time, and the two-key rule.

What if the network is down?

The node keeps a local model and buzzer and stores 30 days of data; alerts fall back to SMS; data is forwarded when the link returns.

Is it a medical device? Is it WHO-certified?

It is an advisory layer beside the certified logger, which stays the official record. Humans decide on stock, following national guidelines. Regulatory status (CDSCO) is part of our next steps.

How will you measure success?

Recall on WHO-grade events, lead time over Nexleaf-style alerts, false alerts per fridge-month, true-alarm suppression below 1%, calibration, cause accuracy, and repair-verification accuracy.

How does it scale and make money?

It reads existing DS01.2 or eVIN data, so scale is software. Per-facility subscription, or bundled with monitoring vendors and state programmes.
