Our answerEight ideas that make ThermoTwin different

Freeze-first

Two-sided prediction, freezing treated as urgent

Most teams will predict "temperature rising". India's measured damage is mostly freezing, which nobody can see afterwards. ThermoTwin forecasts both edges of the 2 to 8 °C band and fast-tracks freeze risk, mirroring WHO's own asymmetry (60 minutes for freeze, 10 hours for heat).

Physics plus ML

A digital twin of every fridge, ML on what physics misses

Each unit gets a small grey-box thermal model whose parameters are learned online. It forecasts physically, and its drifting parameters reveal causes: a leaking gasket, weak cooling, a drifting thermostat. Gradient-boosted trees learn the residual and the risk.

Three clocks

Breach clock, WHO clock, holdover clock

Minutes until the product leaves 2 to 8 °C. Hours until a WHO-grade alarm would fire. During a power cut, hours of holdover left. Staff act on countdowns, not on raw temperatures.

Two-key alerts

ML and physics must agree before anyone's phone rings

A forecast becomes an SMS only when the ML hazard and the twin's own rollout both predict a breach. Clinical studies show multi-signal fusion removed up to 90% of false alarms. WHO-grade rule alarms are never suppressed.

Product, not air

Virtual Vial and the Vaccine Life Meter

Alarms run on a simulated product temperature, like the glycol-buffered probe CDC requires, so door-opening air spikes do not page anyone. A life meter shows rolling 24-hour MKT, vial-monitor-equivalent heat used, and a separate freeze-exposure counter.

Fix loop

From alarm to verified repair

A Cause Card explains what is wrong. A ticket with the evidence goes to the technician. After the repair, the twin checks that cooling strength or seal health is back to baseline and closes the loop: "Repair verified".

Bounded agent

An AI copilot that explains and drafts, but never decides

One orchestrator calls typed tools to write the Cause Card in Hindi or Marathi, guide staff through the SOP, draft maintenance tickets and weekly district digests. Detection and escalation stay deterministic, because agent benchmarks show low root-cause accuracy.

Published scorecard

A replay benchmark nobody else publishes

We run four alerting policies over the same traces (naive threshold, WHO default, Nexleaf-style early alerts, ThermoTwin) and publish lead time, recall on WHO-grade events, true-alarm suppression and false alerts per fridge-month.

The two-key rule

forecast_alert(unit, t) =
      trust(t)            >= 0.7                                   # data is believable
  AND P_breach(<= 30 min) >= theta_budget(unit)                    # ML key, threshold set by the false-alert budget
  AND twin_rollout_as_is crosses the band within 45 min             # physics key
  AND NOT valid_suppression(unit, t)                                # door, restock or defrost window still open

freeze_fast_track(unit, t) =
      trust(t) >= 0.7 AND P(product < 0 C within 30 min) >= theta_freeze   # lower threshold, freeze-first

rule_alerts (never suppressed):  WHO FRZE  product or logger <= -0.5 C for 60 min
                                 WHO HEAT  >= +8 C for 10 h
early rule tiers:                < +2 C for 30 min, > +8 C for 60 min, door open > 5 min, power off with holdover < 4 h

Suppression with re-arm (so delays never hide a real fault)

  • Known contexts open a suppression window: door session, restocking, a scheduled defrost. Only Advisory and Early tiers are suppressed, never Critical.
  • The window length is the unit's own learned recovery time τrec from the twin, times 1.5. If the fridge has not recovered by then, the alert re-arms immediately. This stops a stuck defrost or a door left ajar from hiding behind a delay.
  • Every suppression is logged with its reason. Manual shelving expires automatically after 2 hours and is always listed on the dashboard.

Alarm budget controller

  • On validation traces, θbudget is the lowest threshold that keeps SMS-tier false alerts at or below 1 per fridge per month. That ceiling is our judgement, based on the Belize deployment's real rate of about 0.28 alarms per device per month.
  • The safety counterweight is the true-alarm suppression rate: alerts we hold back that turn out to be real must stay below 1%, the safety margin used by clinical alarm-reduction studies.
  • In deployment, adaptive conformal inference nudges each unit's threshold so its realised false-alert rate tracks the budget.

Feedback loop and cold start

Learning from staff

Every acknowledgement can carry a one-tap tag: restocking, door was open, power cut, technician visit, false alarm. Tags become labels; each week the unit's calibration and suppression windows update. Disagreements between tags and the model are queued for review.

A new fridge on day one

Start from priors for its equipment class (WHO E003 holdover and capacity figures), run the global model and the rule tiers, and identify the twin over the first 48 to 72 hours of normal cycling. Forecast alerts switch on once the twin's 20-minute error is stable.
